3 John is to Gaius, whom the elder loves in truth. It is a private note about hospitality, authority, and a man who wants to be first. The Protestant canon keeps it as a small window on how the early churches treated travelling workers — and how a man who wants control can shut a door the gospel wanted open.

The elder to the beloved Gaius. I pray you may prosper in all things and be in health, as it is well with your soul. Fellow workers have testified to his hospitality, walking in the truth. You do well to send them on their journey in a manner worthy of God, for they have gone out for the name, taking nothing from the Gentiles. The opening is thanks for an open house.

Diotrephes loves to be first, does not acknowledge the apostle’s authority, talks wicked nonsense, refuses the brothers, and puts people out of the church. The elder will come and bring up what he is doing. Demetrius is well spoken of by everyone, and by the truth itself. Two names, two ways of holding a church.

Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends, each by name. What is left open is the visit, Diotrephes’ hold, and whether Gaius will keep the door open. A whole politics of the gospel sits in a note shorter than a chapter of Kings.

Read it as a window, not as a personality test for every difficult member. Hard parts include a public naming and a conflict over who may speak. Famous lines need their setting: loves to be first is a pastoral diagnosis, not a cheap insult; send them on in a manner worthy of God is money, food, and courage, not a sentiment. Greet the friends, each by name, is how a scattered church stays a church. The truth here has a guest room.

1The elder to Gaius the beloved, whom I love in truth.

2Beloved, I pray that you may prosper in all things and be healthy, even as your soul prospers. 3For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and testified about your truth, even as you walk in truth. 4I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth.

5Beloved, you do a faithful work in whatever you accomplish for those who are brothers and strangers. 6They have testified about your love before the assembly. You will do well to send them forward on their journey in a way worthy of God, 7because for the sake of the Name they went out, taking nothing from the Gentiles. 8We therefore ought to receive such, that we may be fellow workers for the truth.

9I wrote to the assembly, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, doesn’t accept what we say. 10Therefore if I come, I will call attention to his deeds which he does, unjustly accusing us with wicked words. Not content with this, neither does he himself receive the brothers, and those who would, he forbids and throws out of the assembly. 11Beloved, don’t imitate that which is evil, but that which is good. He who does good is of God. He who does evil hasn’t seen God. 12Demetrius has the testimony of all, and of the truth itself; yes, we also testify, and you know that our testimony is true.

13I had many things to write to you, but I am unwilling to write to you with ink and pen; 14but I hope to see you soon. Then we will speak face to face. Peace be to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.
