# Chapter 74
## Faith Is Heart And Mind

R. suggested the walk along the ridge where the city became a map and the wind edited speeches before they could pretend to be final.

‘People keep trying to split me,’ she said, hands in coat pockets. Gravel shifted under boots. ‘Either I’m heart—warm, communal, “cultural”—or I’m mind—argumentative, legal, a problem at dinner parties. Both halves are a way of not dealing with a whole person who prays *and* thinks. Halves are convenient for people who only want half a neighbour.’

Michael matched her pace. The ridge path knew them both; it did not take sides. ‘I’ve done the split. On you. On myself. Heart for the bits I like. Mind for the bits I want to win. It’s a dishonest butcher’s cut.’

‘Faith, for me, is not a mood with a book club,’ R. said. ‘And it isn’t a cold exam you pass to join a team. Heart without mind becomes sentiment that folds when history gets hard. Mind without heart becomes a blade that can quote forever and never heal. I’ve met both costumes. I’ve worn both on bad days.’

A kite somewhere cracked its tail like punctuation. Below them a train stitched the valley, ordinary as a sentence that still needed to be lived rather than admired.

Michael thought of Joshua’s amens—felt, not stupid—and Cameron’s sandpaper—smart, not cruel—and his own recent recognition, which had been body and logic and joke in one rush: heat and precision refusing to divorce.

‘My recognition was both,’ he said. ‘If I tell it only as argument, I lie. If I tell it only as heat, I make it unshareable except as cult. The precision was kind because it didn’t need an enemy. Enemies are what half-people collect to feel whole.’

R. glanced at him, wind in her scarf. ‘Keep that. Christians sometimes act as if Muslims left their brains at the door of the mosque. Atheists sometimes act as if believers left their hearts in a fairy tale. I’m tired of being a half-person in other people’s debates. I’m also tired of being “allowed” a mind as if it were a prize. Permission is not respect. Respect notices the mind was always there.’

‘What do you want from me as friend, not as system?’ Michael asked—theory of mind still sore, still in training.

She stopped at the viewpoint. The city glittered with ordinary electricity—streetlights, kitchen windows, the stadium’s distant bowl. ‘When I speak from love of God, don’t translate it immediately into your field theory. Receive it. Let it sit without being useful to you. When I reason, don’t praise me as *surprisingly rational*, as if the baseline were soft. Argue if you must. Don’t pet. Petting is a soft contempt.’

He nodded. The wind made his eyes water; he refused the romance of it being emotion only. It was wind. And emotion. Both. The weather refused the split too.

‘And you?’ R. asked. ‘Heart and mind for you right now? No branding. Inventory.’

Michael watched the train vanish into trees. ‘Heart: I don’t want to lose you to cleverness. I don’t want Harriet to get a loudspeaker instead of a husband. Mind: I don’t want to gut the chain that got me free of hell-as-exit just to keep a peace that isn’t peace. Both: red lines, bigger book, seal respected, recognition not a weapon, sleep as regulation, bin night as test. If it only works on peaks, it isn’t faith. It’s tourism.’

R. smiled sideways. ‘That’s a whole person talking. Rare enough. I’ll take it. I’ll hold you to it when you shrink back into half. You will. I will. The holding is the friendship.’

They walked on. She told him about a commentary she loved—rigorous, devoted, unashamed of either adjective—pages that prayed and argued in the same ink. He told her about Joshua’s shame-into-love without making it a sales deck: brother, cross, the hard comedy of being held when you would rather be right. Disagreement remained on prophecy and seals and what Christ means; it sat in the backpack with the water bottles, carried, not brandished. Carrying is a form of respect.

At the car park she offered him a mint from a tin. ‘Heart and mind,’ she said. ‘If your life is only heart, it’ll curdle into vibe. If it’s only mind, it’ll become a cult of noticing. Keep both. I’ll do the same on my side of the book. Contract without a lawyer.’

‘Contract,’ Michael said, and took the mint as if it were a sacrament of the ordinary, which it was—sweet, small, shared.

Driving back, rain started—mind naming pressure systems, heart glad for the sound on the roof—and Michael let both registers run without electing a president. The wipers kept time. Harriet’s text arrived: *Home soon?* He replied: *Ridge with R. Heart and mind. Both wet.* She sent a thumbs-up, which is a theology of trust when you know the sender.

Faith, he thought, was not a department with a door you could close on either side.

It was the whole animal, walking a ridge, refusing to be halved for anyone’s comfort—including his own, especially his own, because the self is the first butcher who offers to cut you into something more convenient for other people’s ease.
