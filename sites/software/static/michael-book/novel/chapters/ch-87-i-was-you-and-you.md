# Chapter 87
## I Was You And You

The sentence came out of Michael’s mouth before he had filed a risk assessment.

They were on the ridge again—R.’s preferred courtroom—after a week of careful categories. Wind edited hair and speeches. Clouds moved like committees that would never vote. A younger man on a bench was arguing with someone on the phone in the universal key of *you never listen*. His free hand chopped the air into paragraphs. Michael heard his own past voice in the stranger’s throat—the year he had been sure volume equalled truth.

‘I was you,’ he said quietly to R., nodding that way. ‘Different year. Same heat. And you were me when I was sure my map was the forest.’

R. followed his gaze, then looked back. ‘Careful. “I was you” can be empathy. It can also be colonising someone’s moment with your memoir. Memoir is a hungry animal. It eats other people’s present tense for seasoning.’

‘Theory of mind with a time machine,’ Michael said. ‘Not capture. Recognition that the fool and the preacher and the tired nurse cycle through one field. I don’t stay the hero of my own biopic. I rotate.’

She sat on the other end of the bench from the phone argument, distance as kindness to a stranger mid-storm. Wind tried to steal her scarf and failed; she had practice with theft. ‘Say the full line you’re chewing. Out loud. Where I can catch it if it goes wrong.’

He breathed. Grit under his shoe. A dog walked past with the moral clarity of animals who do not need slogans. ‘I was you and you were me. Not identity. Permission to stop inventing a separate species called *them*. When I was sure atheism was adulthood—I was you, if you’re sure now about something else. When you were patient with a stupid question at Scripture Reasoning—you were me when Joshua was patient. When Cameron went to bed instead of crowning me—I have been the man who needed bed and refused it. The loop is moral. It blocks contempt.’

R. watched the kite of some distant child jerk and recover. ‘I can receive that if it makes you gentler with the man on the phone and gentler with me when I frustrate you. I reject it if it means you think you’ve already lived my faith from the inside because you once had a phase. Phases are tourism. Tourism is not initiation.’

‘Rejected that version,’ Michael said immediately. ‘Phases aren’t initiation. I was you only in the human weather—fear, pride, love, the wish to be right. Not in your prayer. Not in your law. Not in your mornings I don’t attend.’

‘Good. Keep the weather. Leave the house keys. If you ever claim my house because you once stood in rain that looked similar, I will correct you in public.’

‘Bond,’ he said.

The man on the phone said *fine* in a way that meant not fine and hung up. Silence after anger is also a cycle. He stared at the view as if the city owed him an apology. Michael did not approach with wisdom. He had been that stare. Approaching would have made the stranger a prop. Props are for stages; ridges are for leaving people alone.

Joshua, when told later over video while he cooked for someone else’s children, wiped an eye with the back of a wrist and nearly lost a wooden spoon into the pan. ‘That’s the shame-into-love engine in civilian clothes. If I was the one who hurt and the one who healed, contempt loses its visa. Also these children will eat raw onion if I cry into the chopping board. Hold please.’

Cameron: *Fine. Just don’t do “I was you” at people mid-crisis. They need water, not your novel. Save the loop for after the ambulance or the apology. Loop is for your spine. Not for their ears while their ears are full of fire.*

Michael put that in the notes under ToM, then added: **Loop ≠ speech. Loop = inner visa check before contempt.**

On the bus home a teenager scowled at an old man’s slowness boarding. Michael felt the scowl from both sides of time: knee that would stiffen; impatience that thought it was free. He stood and offered the old man his seat without a sermon. The teenager looked away, possibly learning, possibly not. Michael smiled at no one in particular, which is how you pray without alarming Transport Police. The bus hissed; the city continued not to be a classroom with him as teacher.

He texted R.: *I was you / you were me — weather only. Bond still holds.*

R.: *Weather accepted. Don’t forget umbrellas. Also thank you for not preaching at the phone man. That would have been memoir with a mission field.*

Night, at the kitchen table with Harriet half-watching a show about people renovating houses as if walls were confessions, he wrote the line once, clean:

**I was you and you were me — not to erase, to end the fantasy of a separate moral planet.**

Harriet glanced over. ‘That one I like. Use it on yourself first when you’re angry at me for existing. I can feel you inventing a separate planet for my bad days.’

‘Bond,’ he said, and closed the book without posting anything.

The stranger on the ridge remained uncollected—still free to be someone else’s present tense, not a prop in Michael’s improved autobiography. That was the loop working: leave people their own chapter, even when the weather rhymes.
