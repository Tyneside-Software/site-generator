# Chapter 8
## Maths, Words, Things

Wednesday afternoon Michael went archaeology-diving in his own laptop and found a fossil with teeth.

He had only meant to recover an old project note—something about a data export from a job so previous it might as well have been carved on clay. Instead he found a folder named, with the bleak poetry of engineers under deadline, **FINAL_final_v3_USE_THIS**.

Inside it, a text file opened like a tomb.

Rows of fields. Not commas. Not tabs. A procession of carets and pipes:

`id^|name^|status^|note`

And then the data, marching:

`1042^|Henderson^|active^|called twice, no answer`  
`1043^|Patel^|closed^|refund issued 14/03`  
`1044^|^|pending^|name missing — fix before Friday`

Michael stared. Somewhere in the building a printer began a long complaint. His coffee, abandoned, formed a skin. On the screen the separators repeated with the patience of a liturgy written by people who just wanted Friday to arrive without a lawsuit.

Joshua answered on the second ring. ‘If this is about the beer mats, I already stuck the sign on the fridge. My housemate asked if Both/And was a craft beer. I said yes in my soul.’

‘I found a delimiter,’ Michael said. ‘Two of them. Caret and pipe. `^|`. We used it because commas lived inside addresses and tabs were untrustworthy and someone in 2009 decided two characters were safer than one.’

‘Romantic,’ Joshua said. ‘Why are you ringing me about punctuation from the Bronze Age?’

Michael scrolled. The pattern repeated, relentless and oddly beautiful. Names. Statuses. Notes that were almost human. Empty fields where a thing should have been and was not—yet the empty was still marked, still held open by the grammar of the line.

‘Because,’ he said slowly, ‘it’s maths and words and things wearing the same overalls. The structure is pure relation—slot, separator, slot. The words are the names we gave the slots. The things are whatever in the world made someone type *Henderson* and *refund issued*. Three layers. One file. No ceremony.’

Joshua made a thoughtful noise. ‘And the missing name on 1044?’

‘Still a row. Absence marked inside a system. Not pure nothing—just a hole with an address. The format refuses to let the hole fall out of the universe. It keeps a seat warm.’

‘You’re going to get emotional about legacy data formats,’ Joshua said. ‘I support this. It’s healthier than getting emotional about either/or street preachers.’

Michael copied a few lines into a scratch buffer and stared until the separators stopped being noise and became architecture. `^|` was not deep magic. It was a decision some tired person had made so that later tired people could still read the world.

‘We pretend maths is cold,’ he said. ‘We pretend words are just air. We pretend things are mute. Then you open a file and find them in a three-legged race, carrying meaning down the corridor without dropping it.’

‘Both/And shop stock,’ Joshua said promptly. ‘Maths *and* words *and* things. Not “pick a tribe: STEM or poetry or bricks.”’

Michael smiled at the screen. ‘The empty name field is my favourite. It’s honest. It says: there is a place where a name should be, and today the place is louder than the name. That’s not failure of reality. That’s a ticket for Friday’s fix.’

‘Did Friday’s fix ever happen?’

Michael checked a newer file, then an email archive, then the sort of spreadsheet that accretes like coral. Time had left fingerprints. Comments. A version history that read like a small moral comedy.

‘Someone put *Unknown* in later,’ he said. ‘Then someone else put a real name. The hole got filled by the ordinary miracle of a person doing their job.’

‘Horizontal beam,’ Joshua said. ‘Again.’

‘You’re going to trademark that phrase.’

‘I’m going to embroider it on a tea towel and hang it in the Both/And shop next to *Superiority — out of stock*.’

Michael saved a copy of the fossil file into a folder called, with new respect, **museum**. He did not clean it up. Cleaning was for systems that still had to run. This one had earned the right to be a specimen—ugly, useful, true.

Before he closed the laptop he wrote in the notebook, under Tuesday’s museum lines:

**Formats are promises: this will still be readable when we are tired.**  
**Empty fields are not pure nothing; they are reserved seats.**  
**Maths, words, things — same file, different jobs.**

On the way home he passed the wall where the glove had been. Still empty. Still a reserved seat of a kind. The high street offered neither preacher nor sceptic tonight, only people buying milk and the smell of rain considering a comeback.

At the corner shop he nearly walked into Joshua, who was emerging with a pint of milk of his own and the look of a man who had planned a collision and called it chance.

‘Tomorrow,’ Joshua said, falling into step. ‘We talk about crosses. Actual ones. Not moods. I’ll bring string. You bring the bit of you that still thinks symbols are only logos.’

Michael nodded. The delimiter sat in his mind like a small, stubborn bridge: two marks holding three kinds of truth in a line so that the world could be said more than one way at once.

‘Load-bearing punctuation,’ he said.

Joshua groaned, fondly. ‘Of course it is. Sleep. The string is patient. You are not.’

Michael walked the last streets with the odd contentment of a man who has remembered that the universe, for all its vastness, still has to choose a delimiter if it wants to say more than one thing at a time.
