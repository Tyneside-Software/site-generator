# Chapter 80
## Under The Pyramid A Builder-God

The image wouldn’t leave: not the glamorous tip, but the underside—the dark, the weight, the question of who holds the holding.

Michael was on his back on the living room floor stretching a tight hip—the least mystical posture available—when the thought arrived with the ceiling cracks for company. Pyramids sit on ground. Ground is not nothing. If the whole is one field, then the ‘under’ is not hell’s basement; it’s still the only room, bearing load. A hairline crack ran toward the light fitting like a river drawn by a child who had heard of maps.

He texted Joshua a photo of the ceiling. *Under the pyramid: builder-god?*

Joshua: *Careful. Don’t invent a new mascot. Also yes: whatever is real includes the labour and the bearing, not only the brand on the peak.*

Michael rolled to sitting. The cat—visiting from a neighbour who travelled—walked across his notes with the confidence of minor deities and sat on the word *capstone* as editorial policy. A pawprint. A veto. Better criticism than most blogs.

Builder-God. The phrase could go wrong in a hundred DIY ways: cosmic bloke with a toolkit; freemason cosplay; the reduction of mystery to construction management; a new way to make himself the foreman of other people’s souls. He did not want a mascot. He wanted a correction to tip-worship.

At Scripture Reasoning that week a passage about creation as speech had already done some work—*calls into being*—and Michael had listened without annexing. Now, alone with a hip flexor and a cat, he tried the thought in public-safe form:

What if God is not only the capstone people fight to name, but the ongoing capacity for worlds to be built, held, repaired? Not a gap in a museum label. A presence in the weight-bearing. The opposite of a sky CEO who outsources the hard bits to middle management called *you*.

He called R., asked if she had five minutes for a metaphor that might be stupid.

‘All metaphors are stupid,’ she said, traffic noise behind her. ‘That’s why we need several. Go. Clock is running.’

He explained the underside, the refusal of tip-only divinity, the fear of mascot, the cat’s veto.

R. was quiet, then: ‘I won’t take Builder-God as a name into my prayer. Names matter. I will take the ethic: don’t love only peaks. In my language God is not a stone among stones. But the warning about prestige-religion—yes. If your recognition makes you a better bearer of weight for other people, keep it. If it makes you the architect of everyone else’s house, bin it. Architects of other houses without invitation are just conquerors with pencils.’

‘That’s the red line in work boots,’ Michael said.

‘Wear them,’ R. said, and hung up to catch a bus, which is a kind of builder-god schedule: arrival, departure, weight of passengers who did not ask for a sermon.

Cameron, emailed the short version, replied: *Also builders can be tyrants. Pharaohs built. The metaphor doesn’t baptise every structure. Check who the pyramid falls on. If it falls on Denise, fix the boiler. Don’t write a hymn.*

Michael wrote that on the notes under the cat’s pawprint.

**Under ≠ automatically holy.**  
**Bearing weight with consent and justice ≠ romanticising pyramids.**  
**Builder as verb more than noun.**

He stood, hip slightly better, and did the verb: fixed the wobbly chair with a screwdriver from the junk drawer; answered a friend’s practical plea with a yes that cost an evening and a train fare; put money in an envelope for someone who would never know his monogram; washed the pan from last night that had become a small archaeology of sauce and delay.

No choir. A click of the chair leg finding honesty. The cat approved by leaving for a sun-patch that required no theology.

Later Joshua dropped by with leftover babysitting cake—too sweet, perfect. They sat on the floor like boys and ate with forks from the tin while the ceiling cracks supervised.

‘Under the pyramid,’ Joshua said, mouth full, ‘is just more world. No escape hatch. That’s your no-outside again. God-as-bearing is another way of saying the field doesn’t outsource the hard bits to a sky CEO. Also this frosting is a theological problem.’

Michael licked frosting from a thumb. ‘I keep almost turning Builder into a CEO anyway. Branding is a disease. I want a logo for the underside. The underside laughs.’

‘Then keep washing plates,’ Joshua said. ‘Plates are anti-CEO technology. So is showing up when knees hurt. So is not photographing your ceiling cracks for the group chat like a mystic postcard.’

‘Already tempted,’ Michael said.

‘I know. That’s why I’m here with cake. Cake is also anti-CEO technology if you share it.’

When Joshua left, Michael looked at the ceiling cracks and blessed them without ceremony. They were only cracks. They were also a diagram of weight and time. He did not photograph them.

Harriet came home to a chair that no longer wobbled and a man who looked mildly proud of gravity.

‘You’ve been useful,’ she said. ‘I can tell. Useful smells different from philosophical.’

‘Builder as verb,’ Michael said.

‘Builder as dishwasher,’ she said. ‘The sink is still a pyramid of dishes. Climb down and start at the base.’

He did. Water ran. The base accepted him without applause. He slept on a mattress that held him without theology, which is the first job of mattresses and possibly of God.

Under everything that looked like a finished shape, mornings kept arriving—and the only god worth trusting, he suspected, was not too proud to be mistaken for the ground.
