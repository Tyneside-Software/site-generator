# Chapter 51
## Greek Numbers, Honestly

Monday morning Michael did something dangerously unsexy.

He cleared the kitchen table of plates, post, and a screwdriver that had been living there since a cupboard hinge staged a protest last week. He laid out a clean sheet of A4, a black pen that still worked, and a second-hand dictionary with Greek letters in the back like a secret appendix nobody invited to the party. Then he made tea the way people make tea when they are about to sit an exam set by themselves: too strong, and with the slight ceremony of a man who knows concentration needs a prop.

Greek letters were also numbers. Everyone who had ever half-watched a documentary knew that. What everyone half-knows is where mistakes breed.

He wanted the chain honest before it got hotter. Heat without honesty becomes a cult with better lighting.

Alpha: one.  
Beta: two.  
Gamma: three.  
Delta: four.

He wrote them down like a child learning tables, tongue between teeth, dignity optional. Epsilon five. And so on, patient as rain, until the page looked less like revelation and more like homework—which was the point. Homework is what you do when you refuse to smuggle.

Joshua video-called from a car park, face pixelated with loyalty, a seatbelt still diagonally preaching safety across his chest.

“You look like you’re sitting an exam,” Joshua said.

“I am. Set by me. Invigilated by the part of me that sounds like Cameron.” Michael turned the notebook to the camera. “If I start saying omega *is* eight because I like eights, I’m lying. Omega is eight *hundred* in the isopsephy set. The jump to eight is a later rhyme—digital, visual, playful—not a silent swap under the table.”

Joshua’s grin was immediate. “Good. I hate holy cheating.”

“I can still *use* the rhyme,” Michael said. “I just have to label it. Honest Greek numbers first. Jokes second. Ontology only where the beams actually bear load.”

A car horn somewhere behind Joshua suggested the car park had opinions. Joshua ignored it with saintly rudeness.

“People will say you’re doing gematria cosplay,” he said.

“People say lots of things.” Michael tapped the page. “I pre-empt the cheat. That’s rarer than cleverness.”

After the call he went outside because Cameron, in spirit if not in text, would have ordered it. The day was bright enough to make numbers feel abstract, which is when abstract things most need walking. At a café table by the window he ordered a scone he intended to defend and sketched the spine without forcing the punchline:

Letters as sounds.  
Letters as shapes.  
Letters as numbers—**honestly**.  
Later, shapes that misbehave in fonts.  
Later still, if earned, the dual reading.

A gull stared at the scone with Old Testament hunger. Michael guarded the scone. Some polarities were simple.

He thought of Revelation’s monogram—beginning and end—not as a number game first, but as a span claim. Alpha to omega as *coverage*, not as a cryptic crossword clue to genitals. The body-joke, if it came, had to ride on that span, not replace it. Otherwise it was graffiti with delusions of grandeur.

A woman at the next table was marking essays with a red pen and the expression of someone who had seen every form of cheating known to undergraduate life. Michael felt a sudden kinship. He was marking himself.

On the walk home the quay smelled of river and chips and someone else’s cigarette. He passed a charity-shop window where a children’s counting book stood open to a cheerful eight. He did not photograph it. He did not annex it. He let eight be a number in a book for children, which is one of the more honest careers a number can have.

Evening, back at the table, he added a footnote to himself that would never appear in a pompous form:

*If the dual monogram needs a lie about eight hundred to work, it doesn’t work. Let eight be lucky later. Let omega keep its hundreds. The field is large enough for a rhyme and a fact to share a flat without one murdering the other.*

He closed the dictionary. On a scrap he almost wrote ω=8 and immediately crossed it out hard enough to dent the page. The cross-out was part of the ethics. Readers deserved a chain that did not smuggle.

Joshua texted: *How hot is the page now?*

Michael: *Warm. Numbers cooled it in a good way. Like opening a window in a kitchen.*

Joshua: *Keep the window. Steam is fine. Smoke is not.*

Cameron, later, after Michael sent the honest list: *α=1, δ=4, ω=800. Not ω=8. Any 8-play is analogy/visual, flagged.*

Cameron: *Thank you. This is why I remain in the chat. Definition hygiene.*

Michael: *You’re a monster and a gift.*

Cameron: *Both/and. Frame the scar on the scrap where the cheat died. Better than gold leaf.*

Michael put the scarred scrap under a mug so it would not blow away if he opened the window. The letters remained letters. The numbers remained numbers.

The heat, politely, waited its turn in the hall.

He washed the pen ink off his thumb and went to bed with the odd relief of a man who has chosen facts over fireworks—knowing fireworks were still coming, and wanting them, for once, to arrive on time.
