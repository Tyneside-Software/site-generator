# Chapter 81
## Christ Double-Equals God

He said it first to Joshua, because some sentences need a brother before they need a public.

They were in the car park after buying too much milk—always milk, the sacrament of continuation—when Michael put the bag down on the bonnet and spoke. A trolley rattled past with a wheel that believed in suffering. The sky was ordinary grey. Ordinary is a good studio for scandals.

‘For me, Christ double-equals God,’ Michael said. ‘Not mascot. Not good teacher only. The claim is the scandal. If I dilute it to keep the interfaith weather nice, I’m lying about the beam that taught me shame-into-love. I’m lying about your amen. I’m lying about the cross that isn’t only a plus sign on a beer mat.’

Joshua rested a hand on the cold metal of the bonnet. ‘And R.?’

‘Gets the truth without the sales pitch. I’ve been so careful not to capture her that I almost edited myself into a Unitarian smoothie. That’s not respect. That’s fear in a polite jumper. Red lines include not managing her with a softer false self.’

Joshua’s mouth twitched. ‘Say the operator again. Out loud. Like you mean the keys.’

‘Double-equals,’ Michael said. ‘==. Same type, same claim, programming joke included. Christ == God. Not a lower-case approximation. And also—because I’ve learned fonts and friends—my saying it doesn’t force her CPU to compile it. Confession is not capture. Capture is when I treat her no as a bug.’

‘Good,’ Joshua said. ‘Hold the scandal. Hold the non-coercion. Both. Now put the milk in the fridge before it becomes a metaphor for delayed courage.’

They delivered milk to the fridge like men on a holy errand. Harriet’s note on the door said *eggs*. Michael added eggs to the list of things that were not God and still mattered. Then he rang R. and asked for a walk that would not be ambush—ridge path, daylight, no napkin as visual aid.

She heard him out with the wind editing their hair. Boots on grit. A crow made a noise like torn paper.

‘I’m not asking you to agree,’ he said. ‘I’m telling you what I actually think, because red-line respect includes not managing you with a softer false self. Christ == God is my spine, not my lasso. If that ends the meal someday, it ends clean. I’d rather lose a fake peace than keep a fake me.’

R. walked several steps in silence. Michael did not fill it with more operators.

‘Thank you for not packaging it as “what Muslims secretly believe if they were consistent,”’ she said at last. ‘I’ve heard that insult in academic clothing. What you said is *your* confession. I can receive a confession without signing it. Receiving is not converting. Do not put receiving in your diary as almost.’

‘Can we stay friends with the operator on the table?’

‘We already are,’ R. said. ‘My shahada isn’t a debate club position. Your Christology isn’t a hobby. We don’t average them into a beige god. We keep eating with each other. That’s the miracle the committee didn’t schedule. Committees prefer beige. I don’t.’

Michael felt relief so sharp it was almost grief. ‘I was afraid honesty would end the bigger book.’

‘Honesty ends fake books,’ R. said. ‘Those should end. Real books can hold a spine and a friendship without stapling them into one pamphlet.’

They reached the bench that looked toward the football stadium, lit for a match like a temporary cathedral. Crowds would sing soon. Michael told her, carefully, that the monogram didn’t replace Christ == God; it sat in the same chain as generative life, not as a rewrite of the gospel into pure geometry. Geometry had been a teacher. It was not the whole school.

‘I’ll trust you until you give me reason not to,’ R. said. ‘If you start treating my no as incomplete maths, we’re done. If you hold == as love’s claim and not as a scoreboard, we continue. Scoreboards are for sport. This is not sport.’

‘Deal.’

Joshua joined them later with chips in paper, the true ecumenical food, salt on his fingers like a blessing that didn’t need a committee. He raised a chip like a candle. ‘To non-beige truth and non-tyrannical truth.’

‘To operators that don’t force run-time on other machines,’ Michael said.

R. laughed. ‘You’re such a nerd. It’s why you’re safe-ish. Stay safe-ish. Dangerous nerds write other people’s code without permission.’

They ate under the stadium glow. Somewhere a crowd sang a name that was not God’s and felt like belonging anyway. Michael did not annex the song. He did not annex R.’s silence between chips. He sat with the double-equals in his chest—heavy, bright, unweaponised for one evening—

—and let the claim be true without requiring everyone else’s signature to make it real. Scandals are only tyrannies when they need an audience of converts. Tonight the audience was salt, paper, friends, and a sky that stayed grey on purpose.
