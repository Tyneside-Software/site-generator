# Chapter 62
## Fuck Life / Fucked By Life

Joshua said it first, because some amens need a brother’s mouth.

They were on the low wall outside the community hall. Light off. Side door locked. The street doing its evening shuffle—bags, bikes, a child arguing with a scooter about physics. Michael had been talking too carefully, still afraid the dual monogram would be mistaken for contempt, still afraid no-sin would be mistaken for a shrug. Careful is a kind of armour. Armour is heavy on a mild night.

Joshua bumped his shoulder. “We can enjoy being fucked by life,” he said softly, “and we can fuck life.”

Michael stared.

“Both,” Joshua added, deadpan and warm. “Active and receptive. Make and be made. The generative poles in verbs you can’t put on a Sunday school wall without a meeting. I’m not performing shock. I’m translating the heartbeat into English people actually speak when the plaster comes off.”

Michael’s laugh came out cracked. “You’re going to get us banned from somewhere.”

“Worth it,” Joshua said. “Listen: *fucked by life* is the honest receiving—loss, surprise, time, other people’s wills, the body’s limits, grace you didn’t schedule. *Fuck life* is the honest making—agency, art, repair, refusal, love that builds, the hard path chosen without martyr cosplay. Either alone becomes a pose. Victim-only or conqueror-only. The field is both.”

Wind moved a crisp packet in liturgical circles. Somewhere a television laughed at a studio cue. The hall behind them kept its silence like a closed eye that still knew what light was.

Michael tried the verbs on memories like coats. The night of layers, when despair had entered him and enjoyment had, somehow, answered. The recruiter’s fork. The plate-washing crisis. Goosebumps at a sign. The napkin on a pub table that did not care about dignity. He had been entered by events. He had also made—sentences, apologies, a door opened, a monogram drawn in biro that skipped on the cheap paper.

He sent Cameron a line, because some truths need a witness who will not clap:

*Josh line: fuck life / fucked by life. Both. Warm adult. Not nihilism.*

Cameron: *I left for one afternoon.*

Michael: *You can stay left if you need.*

Cameron: *…I’m here. It’s true. It’s also going on a list titled phrases I will not repeat at work.*

Michael: *Fair.*

They walked. Streetlights made honest pools. Michael felt the last Victorian ghost try to faint and escorted it politely toward the kerb.

“I don’t have a big dick and I am God,” Joshua said suddenly, so mild it took a second to arrive.

Michael stopped walking. “Excuse me?”

Joshua’s grin was pure brother. “Both-and. Size and totality aren’t a single axis. Length isn’t the preferred direction—you wrote that. I can be limited in one measurement and not exiled from the whole. So can you. So can anyone reading. The brag and the shame both lose their cosmic lease.”

Michael wiped an eye, laughing. “That’s going in the book.”

“I should charge royalties.”

“You get tea.”

“I accept tea.”

They made tea at Michael’s, because treaties need steam. The monogram watched from its pin like a quiet relative who has already heard the family’s worst jokes and stayed for pudding. Warm adult was the register: not sneering, not simpering, not trying to hurt the reader with edge. Just true about how lives go in and out of power with the world—fucked by the news, fucking a path through it; fucked by illness, fucking a day into shape anyway; fucked by love’s arrivals, fucking a life open to receive them without pretending control was the point.

“Does this cheapen the no-sin clarity?” Michael asked, hands around the mug.

“Only if you use it to avoid grief,” Joshua said. “I’m not telling you to thrust at a funeral. I’m telling you the same field that breaks you also lets you answer. That’s not cheap. That’s costly hope.”

Michael nodded. Costly hope he could carry. Hope that never costs anything is a brochure. He thought of the clarity still humming under his ribs from the kitchen floor days—no exile, no pure elsewhere—and how these verbs did not cancel it. They inhabited it. The field that will not throw you out is also the field that will not stop happening to you, or stop needing you to answer.

At the door, saying goodnight, Joshua added, “Tomorrow: has / is / maybe. Uncollapsed. Don’t rush the wave.”

“I won’t.”

After the door closed Michael stood in the hall and said the couplet once, quietly, like a prayer with mud on its boots. Fuck life. Fucked by life. Both. Agency and receptivity without renting a pure spiritual dialect that never sweats.

He thought of readers who would flinch at the verbs and readers who would only relax when they heard them—two congregations, same field. The chapter’s job was not to shock the first or flatter the second. It was to tell the truth about power with the world without converting either half into a lifestyle brand.

The hall did not collapse.

Neither did he.

Outside, a car door slammed. Someone swore mildly at keys. Life, fucking and being fucked by itself in the ordinary key, continued—and for once Michael did not need a cleaner vocabulary to stay inside the only room.
