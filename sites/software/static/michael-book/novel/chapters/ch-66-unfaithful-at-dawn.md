# Chapter 66
## Unfaithful At Dawn

The stereo clock said 06:08 with the smug certainty of machines that have never been caught in a joke.

Michael had not slept so much as docked. The monogram still lived on yesterday’s napkin, folded in his wallet like theology that might set off a metal detector. The last line still rang under his ribs—*It’s a funny old life*—and the morning had the gall to arrive anyway: bins, a blackbird practising arrogance, a delivery van reversing as if the universe had never heard of Alpha.

He put music on because silence after recognition can feel like a dare you are too tired to accept.

Exit Eden. *Unfaithful.* The first guitar line sliced the grey kitchen light into ribbons. A woman sang betrayal as if it were weather—inevitable, specific, hard to mop once it had got into the skirting.

Michael stood in yesterday’s shirt, bare feet on cold laminate, and almost laughed.

Not because the song was funny. Because irony had walked in without wiping its boots. He had spent a week arguing that sin-as-elsewhere was a booking error; that the field had no pure outside; that faithfulness and unfaithfulness were dramas *inside* the only room there was. And here was a metal ballad about cheating hearts, timed to the minute, while the kettle clicked and the same floor that had started the whole chain waited for socks.

‘You’re early,’ he told the speakers.

The speakers did not apologise. Speakers never do.

He turned the volume down a notch so the neighbours would not have to share his ontology before coffee. The chorus rose anyway. Something in him flinched—not guilt as a courtroom, more the body’s memory of vows. Old vows. Half-kept ones. The small daily unfaithfulnesses: attention sold to screens, kindness postponed, truth bent into a better story about himself.

Joshua would still be asleep, face in a pillow, conscience off-duty until toast. Cameron would be horizontal on principle until nine. R. would be somewhere in her own morning—prayers Michael did not own and would not translate into his private monogram for sport.

He poured the tea. Steam rose like a modest ghost. The song kept accusing someone of something.

‘I’m not cheating on God,’ he said to the empty room, then winced, because saying it aloud made him sound like a man who needed a solicitor and a better alibi.

He meant: the joke had not been permission. No-sin-as-outside was not a hall pass for cruelty. The monogram was generative, not predatory. If anything, the recognition had made unkindness *worse*—not because hell had been reinstated under new management, but because harm had nowhere left to hide as someone else’s problem.

The track hit 06:08 in his head even though the clock had already moved on. Times stuck when they wanted to. Appointed hours had been a theme all week; he was not going to make this one a shrine. Still. Six and eight. The numbers sat in the air like keys waiting to be pressed by a man who ought to know better.

He walked to the desk. The keyboard slept under a thin dust of yesterday’s triumph. Keys six and seven still shared their old fence. Key eight waited, smug as a spare joke, for whatever hardware farce the universe had queued next.

Michael did not press anything yet. He listened to the last chorus, mug warm, recognition still warm too, and felt the peculiar loneliness of a man who has finished a book and discovered that the next morning still requires socks, and possibly deodorant, and almost certainly the washing-up.

The song ended. Another track tried to start—something brighter, less interested in moral weather. He stopped it with a thumb that felt suddenly responsible.

Quiet returned, ordinary as bread.

He texted Joshua: *Woke to Unfaithful at 06:08. Irony doing cardio.*

Three dots. A pause long enough to suggest Joshua was vertical enough to type.

*God has a playlist. Don’t marry the timestamp.*

Michael smiled into the empty kitchen. He set the mug down, looked at the cold floor, and said the line again under his breath—not as an ending now, but as a weather report he was learning to live inside without turning the barometer into a reliquary.

Outside, dawn finished arriving without a press release. A fox cut across the car park with the economy of someone who had never needed a metaphor. Inside, the napkin in his wallet held α==ω like a secret that refused to stay only clever.

He washed the mug. He opened the curtains. Light laid a pale stripe across the laminate and did not ask permission.

He did not solve faithfulness in one song. He did not crown the clock. He only noticed that the first morning after recognition had chosen a title track about betrayal—and that even that, somehow, still belonged to the funny old life, which was not done with him yet, and which, if he was honest, he was not done with either.

He put on socks. The laminate forgave him. Somewhere a bin lorry made the world official.

Michael left the kitchen light on a moment longer than necessary, as if the room had earned a small courtesy, then switched it off and went to face the day that had already started without waiting for him to finish being profound.
