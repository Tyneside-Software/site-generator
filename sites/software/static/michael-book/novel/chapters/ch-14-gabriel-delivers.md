# Chapter 14
## Gabriel Delivers

Saturday morning arrived without a brass section.

Michael woke to grey light and the awareness of his own name still sitting on the bedside table of his mind like a glass of water from the night before. *Who is like God?*—a question-shaped label, a shove away from thrones. He made tea. He did not argue with chairs. Progress.

His phone buzzed while the kettle worked.

Unknown number. Then, as the message preview slid up, not unknown after all—an old friend-of-a-friend thread, a group chat that had been dormant so long it had practically fossilised, the kind of digital drawer where people go to become Christmas round-robins.

The message was from Gabriel.

Not an archangel. A human Gabriel: works in logistics, laughs with his whole face, once helped Michael move a sofa up a stairwell that violated several laws of physics and good sense. They had not spoken in months. Life had done that thing life does where it piles boxes in the doorway of intentions.

The text said:

**Keep going.**

That was all.

No emoji. No explanation. No *hope you’re well* throat-clearing. Just two words, delivered with the blunt grace of a parcel left on the correct step.

Michael sat down hard enough that the Chair of Being—formerly Non-Being, reformed by buttocks and time—creaked a welcome.

He typed: **??**  
Deleted it.  
Typed: **You too.**  
Deleted that too, because it sounded like a poster in a gym.

He rang Joshua.

‘Gabriel texted me *Keep going*,’ Michael said. ‘Out of nowhere. From a dead group chat. As if he’d been reading the minutes of my week.’

Joshua was quiet long enough for Michael to hear toast being chewed. ‘Had you told him any of it?’

‘No. We haven’t spoken since—what—spring? Sofa era feels like another geological period.’

‘Then you have two honest options,’ Joshua said. ‘One: coincidence, brain pattern-matching, the museum accepting a random exhibit and you hanging a plaque on it. Two: the field is friendlier than your sarcasm. Either way, the instruction is good. Keep going is not *become a prophet*. It’s *don’t put the tools down just because the first wall looks straight*.’

Michael stared at the two words until they blurred. ‘Gabriel. Messenger name. I’m trying not to get drunk on that.’

‘Don’t,’ Joshua said firmly. ‘You just spent last night learning your own name is a question that blocks thrones. Don’t build a throne for a text message. Receive it like a sandwich from a friend. Nourishing. Not a coronation.’

‘How did he even—’

‘Maybe he dreamed you looked tired. Maybe someone mentioned you. Maybe his thumb slipped on an old intention. You can ask him later without turning it into a relic. For now, answer like a person.’

Michael hung up, breathed, and wrote back:

**Thanks. Needed that more than is dignified to admit. How are you?**

Three minutes later the reply landed with the same unfussy weight:

**Tired. Moving house again. Saw your name in the chat list and thought of the sofa and your stubborn face. Keep going means keep going. Not only you. Me too.**

Michael laughed—soft, relieved, human. The cosmos could keep its brass section. This was better: two men in different postcodes, both tired, both still in the only room there is, passing a small coal of encouragement so it wouldn’t go out.

He sent: **Sofa PTSD never left. If you need a horizontal beam that lifts, call me.**

Gabriel: **I might. Go eat breakfast.**

Michael ate breakfast. Eggs. Toast that did not burn. Tea that did not go cold while he argued with absence, because for once he was not arguing with absence—he was chewing.

He opened the notebook and wrote, under the name-lines:

**Gabriel delivers (human department).**  
**Message: Keep going.**  
**Meaning: not a throne. A sandwich. Shared.**  
**Answer: thank you, and same to you, and the sofa stands ready.**

Mid-morning he walked to the corner shop for milk he actually needed. The high street was Saturday-busy: prams, football shirts, a charity collector with a smile of professional hope. No amplifier preacher. No loud sceptic. Just the museum in casual clothes, accepting donations of noise and weather.

His phone buzzed once more—Joshua this time, voice not text, because some kindnesses prefer air.

‘Hellfire Club research this afternoon?’ Joshua said. ‘Purely historical. I bring cake. You bring scepticism. Act carefully with signs. Act generously with friends.’

‘Deal,’ Michael said. ‘Keep going applies to cake.’

‘Especially to cake.’

He walked home with the milk and the odd buoyancy of a man who has been told, by someone not trying to convert him, that the week is allowed to continue. Not because the bricks were finished. Because unfinished is not the same as failed.

*Keep going.*

Two words. Load-bearing. Horizontal. Delivered without wings—

—which was, Michael thought, rather the style of the whole business so far.
