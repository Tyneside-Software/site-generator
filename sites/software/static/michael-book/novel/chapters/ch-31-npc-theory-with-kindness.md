# Chapter 31
## NPC Theory (With Kindness)

The next afternoon Michael sat in a café that pretended to be European and was really just Geordie with better cups.

He had come to work. The work was open on his laptop. The laptop was open on the work. Between them, the problem of other people kept sitting down uninvited.

At the next table a man repeated the same three opinions he had heard on the radio that morning, in the same order, with the same punchline. At the counter a woman ordered her usual as if the script had been laminated years ago. Outside, traffic obeyed lights. Inside, a barista moved through the choreography of steam and names written wrong on cardboard.

Michael's brain, fed too much philosophy and not enough sleep, offered him a word floating through culture like a meme with a dagger: *NPC*.

Non-player character. Background code. Dialogue tree with six options. Half the internet had used it meaner than any game designer intended. It was a way of saying: some people do not seem fully on.

Cruelty booked the early flight, as cruelty always does. Then the other thing arrived—the thing that made him put his pen down and actually look.

Energy is finite. Attention is finite. The brain burns fuel like a small furnace and still cannot model everything at full fidelity. If high-resolution agency—long horizons, novel choice, the expensive work of thinking a thought that has not been downloaded—costs free energy, then a system with a budget cannot give everyone a cinematic character arc at once.

Most of the world has to run on cheaper scripts. Not as insult. As engineering.

He watched the radio man finish his coffee. The man was not empty. He had loved people, paid bills, known grief, chosen socks. The script was not the whole person. The script was how a finite creature survives Tuesday without inventing civilisation from first principles every morning.

Joshua arrived late, coat still deciding whether it was winter, and ordered something complicated because he enjoyed making baristas invent.

'You're staring at civilians like a detective,' Joshua said.

'I'm trying not to be a bastard about NPCs.'

'Ah. The video-game insult.'

'It can be. Or thermodynamics wearing a joke.' Michael tapped the table. 'Finite energy. Concentrated agency. Background processes. If the universe is computing itself under a power limit, most nodes run cheap.'

Joshua's mouth did the small brother-smile that meant *not wrong, about to overshoot*.

'And the overshoot is treating cheap scripts as proof someone isn't real.'

'Yes.'

'So what's the kindness version?'

Michael looked at the radio man, laughing at his own punchline again, and felt something like affection for the loop. 'Most people are running the efficient code that keeps the world from catching fire. High agency is expensive. It doesn't make you a higher species. It makes you a costly subprocess with a responsibility not to sneer at the rest of the render.'

Joshua nodded. 'Also: you are sometimes an NPC in someone else's story. You buy milk. You say *fine, thanks*. You follow the road markings. Welcome to the background cast of billions.'

Michael almost argued, then didn't. The argument would have been vanity wearing work boots.

Cameron appeared in the doorway with the timing of a man who could smell a ladder being assembled from three streets away. He sat without ordering—efficiency or judgement; both possible.

'If you're about to call half of Newcastle NPCs,' he said, 'I'm going to bed early again.'

'With kindness. Finite compute. No moral ladder.'

'Still sounds like a ladder. Soft edges. Still a ladder.'

Joshua laughed into his complicated drink. 'He's not wrong. Keep the physics. Throw away the ranking. People are modes of the same experience. Some noisy and open-ended. Some quiet and scripted. None of them outside the only room.'

Outside, a bus sighed. Inside, the radio man left a tip that was neither generous nor mean—just the usual, which is how civilisations stay upright.

Michael thought of late tabs the night before, of Cameron asleep on principle, of his own mornings on autopilot—teeth, keys, the same route to the same desk. High agency was a weather system, not a permanent rank. Some hours he was the expensive subprocess. Some hours he was the man ordering the usual without invention. Both could be true without a moral ladder under his feet.

A woman at the window table closed a paperback, checked the time, and left with the quiet competence of someone whose day had already spent its surplus on other people's needs. Michael watched her go without inventing a story in which she was unfinished. That, he decided, was the kindness version in practice: not a theory. A refusal.

Joshua stood. 'Kindness version means you still build hard things. You just stop needing contempt as fuel.'

'Contempt is cheap fuel. Burns dirty.'

'There you go.'

Cameron checked his watch, not rudely. 'I'm going. Build hard things. Don't recruit me into your render hierarchy.'

'Wouldn't dare.'

Michael finished his coffee.

It was ordinary.

That was the point.
