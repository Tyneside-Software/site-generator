# Chapter 77
## Empire Religions And The Under-Radar

The museum had a room for empire that tried, with modern labels, not to lie too much.

Michael went alone on a grey afternoon because some lessons require no witness to perform for. He paid the entrance fee with a card that made a cheerful noise inappropriate to swords. Glass cases held coins, missionary tracts, trade beads, a Bible with a soldier’s name in the front, a Qur’an fragment with a catalogue number that made R.’s voice start up in his head: *object, not person—careful*.

He stood a long time at a map where colours bled across continents like spilled jam. A child asked her father what the pink meant; the father said ‘us’ before catching himself and saying ‘complicated’. Michael almost hugged the correction.

Empire religions—he used the phrase in his notes without loving it—were not only hypocrisy. They were also grandmothers, poetry, law, hospitals, genuine awe. Reducing them to conquest was a different conquest, performed by a man with a pencil. Still. The buildings that survived had often been backed by armies or taxes or the quiet violence of ‘default’. Default is a soft empire. It asks fewer questions because the forms already know the answer.

Under-radar paths had fewer cases. Folklore corners. A sentence about suppression. A reconstructed amulet behind glass that made secrecy look like a hobby. The museum’s silence around what could not be displayed was itself a kind of exhibit. Michael sat on a bench until his legs stopped performing *serious visitor*.

A school group flooded past, loud with packet crisps and the eternal present of childhood. History would reach them later, or not, depending on teachers and algorithms. One child wore a sticker that said *I DIG THE PAST*. Michael hoped the past would dig back gently.

His phone buzzed. Joshua: *You in the empire room? Don’t become a professional cynic. Cynicism is just empire for people who lost.*

Michael: *Trying to hold both. Power shapes which gods get loudspeakers.*

Joshua: *Yes. Also: the powerless can be cruel. The powerful can be saints. Keep the lens, lose the cartoon.*

He met R. after, as planned, in the museum café where the cake was dry and the honesty easier for it. A tourist photographed the cake as if it were an artefact. R. broke a scone that fought back.

‘I kept thinking of your seal and my bigger book,’ Michael said. ‘Then I felt sick, because even our private argument sits on top of histories where people didn’t get to argue—they got to convert, pay, leave, or die.’

‘Good sick,’ R. said. ‘Stay with it without drowning. My faith isn’t only empire. Your Christian inheritance isn’t only empire. But if we talk interfaith as if it were a yoga class of equals, we erase blood. Scripture Reasoning is a modern mercy built after modern crimes, not instead of them.’

Michael nodded, fork idle. ‘Under-radar isn’t automatically innocent. Empire isn’t automatically fake. The question is who gets the microphone, and who has to whisper, and whether my recognition is a microphone I’m pointing at other people’s faces.’

‘Now you’re thinking like a man with neighbours,’ R. said. ‘Not a man with a theory-shaped mirror.’

‘I used to want a clean lineage for the truth,’ he said. ‘Clean lineages are a luxury belief. Most truth arrives with muddy boots and a problematic uncle.’

‘Including mine,’ R. said. ‘Including yours. Welcome to adulthood. It doesn’t come with a gift shop.’

She tore another piece of scone, smaller this time, as if practice improved the fight. ‘When you leave here, don’t write a manifesto about empire. Write a rule about your own mouth. Loudspeakers are not proofs. Whispers are not proofs. Attention is the ethic.’

‘My recognition is not a colony,’ Michael said.

‘That’s the rule,’ she said. ‘Say it when you’re tempted to perform me as evidence. Say it when a room rewards synthesis. Say it when the napkin feels like a flag.’

He walked her to the tram stop under a sky the colour of old display cabinets. A busker played something almost a hymn and almost a football chant; the city specialised in almosts. On the tram home alone he watched the routes: mosque, church, gym, betting shop, all claiming forms of hope. Under-radar lives moved between them—nurses on night shift, kids with headphones, a woman counting coins at the stop with the focus of prayer. No one asked him for a monogram.

He wrote in the notes app:

**Loudspeakers are not proofs. Whispers are not proofs. Attention is the ethic.**  
**My recognition is not a colony.**

When he got down, the evening had the colour of old coins. He bought milk because Joshua had ordered milk as medicine for cleverness. At the till the cashier said ‘You all right?’ in the way that means *I see a face*. He said yes and meant *working on it*.

Cameron, later: *Empire room is fine. Just don’t come home as a priest of guilt. Guilt is also a loudspeaker. Buy milk. Wash a plate. Stay mammal.*

Michael washed a plate. The plate did not award him a medal for historical awareness. It simply became clean, which was more than most theories managed in an afternoon.

Joshua rang while the kettle boiled. ‘How was the empire?’

‘Still standing,’ Michael said. ‘I didn’t crown myself anti-emperor. That’s progress.’

‘Anti-emperors become emperors with better slogans,’ Joshua said. ‘Milk?’

‘Bought.’

‘Bond.’

He did not solve empire.

He only refused, for one more day, to let a private recognition become a little empire of noticing with himself as quiet emperor—which is harder than it sounds when treasure still lives in a wallet, and the wallet still lives in a city built by people who never asked his permission to be complicated.
