# Chapter 28
## Both And Not Both

Thursday Michael wrote too much.

It began as a clean idea: ^ for *both* (the updraft, the inclusion, the yes-and), & for *and* in plain sight—or, if you squinted with programmer eyes, for tricksy logics where *and* and *not* danced. He had meant a short note. The note became an essay. The essay became a cathedral built out of two characters and caffeine.

*Both and not both,* he typed, and felt the thrill of a phrase that sounded like it could open locks.

By paragraph six he was claiming polysemy as a lifestyle. By paragraph twelve the keyboard symbols had acquired something uncomfortably like destiny. He stopped short of crowning them The Answer—Cameron's threat about early bedtime still glowed in memory—but he could see the crown on the table, waiting.

He sent the draft to Joshua and Cameron anyway, because secrecy is how private cults begin.

Joshua rang first. 'Beautiful overshoot. Save the heat. You're describing how words carry more than one passenger. Don't make ^& hire a PR firm.'

Cameron arrived at the café where Michael was already waiting, laptop open like a confession.

'You saw it,' Michael said.

'I saw you almost crown a typography accident. Also some true things about how meanings stack. Polysemy is real. People use one word for five jobs and then fight wars over which job is official. That part I'll buy. The part where shift-six and shift-seven become the secret name of God—return to sender.'

Michael sat. 'Fair. I got drunk on adjacency.'

'You get drunk on adjacency weekly,' Cameron said, not unkind. 'It's why you're interesting and why you need friends with bedtimes. Both/and is a good shop. Both-and-not-both is a fine riddle. Neither is a licence to smuggle a bailey behind a motte and call the smuggling depth.'

He laid it out with the precision of a man removing a speck while holding a plank of his own.

'Motte: "haha two keys are funny neighbours, language is dual, lighten up." Bailey: "actually this glyph pair is the structure of reality and if you don't see it you're asleep." Stay in the motte when I'm in the room. Visit the bailey on your own time and admit when you've moved house.'

Michael read the shape of it twice. The sting was clean. The care under it was cleaner.

He thought of the Both/And beer mat from week one, of wave and point sharing a pub without a wedding certificate from the Absolute. 'So the pin is: funny dual, useful reminder, not the final boss.'

'The pin is: say what you mean at the strength you can defend. If you mean "these symbols help me remember inclusion and tension," say that. If you mean "I solved the universe with a keyboard," expect me to go home and sleep the sleep of the unimpressed.'

Joshua's line arrived into the silence; Michael showed Cameron the phone: *Listen to the sandpaper. Then keep the essay's true middle and delete the throne room.*

Michael opened the draft and began cutting. Out went the destiny talk. Out went the implied superiority toward people who just typed ampersands in company names. In stayed the comedy of neighbours. In stayed polysemy—the honest mess of words doing double shifts. In stayed a single careful line he read aloud:

'^& can mean more than one true thing without any one meaning owning the police.'

It still crackled. It no longer demanded a salute. Cameron read the revised paragraph over his shoulder. 'Better. Still weird. Weird is allowed. Bailey-smuggling is not.'

'How do I know when I've moved house?'

'When your tone goes from *look at this odd neighbour* to *why don't you live here yet*. Watch your verbs. Watch whether disagreement makes you curious or grand.'

'Thanks for the pin.'

'Thanks for not doubling down into a cult. Tomorrow I'm going to bed at a human hour. If you invent another glyph religion overnight, leave a message after the tone. I bill for exorcisms in sandwiches.'

They parted with a half-hug that pretended to be a handshake. On the walk home Michael passed a shop sign: *Fish & Chips*. The ampersand did its job without metaphysics. He tipped an imaginary hat to it and felt the bailey urge sulk, which was progress.

A child on a scooter shouted *and* at nothing particular—joining two thoughts that would never make a dissertation—and the word did its glue-work in the air and vanished. Michael laughed once, privately. That was the strength he could defend: neighbours, reminders, comedy first.

That night he retitled the note *Both And Not Both (Working, Not Crowned)* and slept without the fever of having almost founded a church of two keys. He dreamed, mildly, of a motte with a nice view and a bailey he visited only with a packed lunch and a return ticket.

The symbols remained on the keyboard, patient as plastic.

They did not need his coronation.

They needed his restraint—and his friends.
