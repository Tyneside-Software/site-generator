# Chapter 70
## The Final Chapter Argument

It started, as important arguments often do, over washing-up.

Michael had cooked—pasta of the optimistic kind, sauce from a jar that claimed rustic heritage while being born in a factory. R. had brought salad and the sort of bread that makes people behave. Joshua had been invited, then delayed by a work call; his empty plate waited like a diplomatic seat that still believed in diplomacy.

They were alone in Michael’s kitchen with the radio low and the window open to a cool evening. Soap on the pan. Steam on the glass. The domestic sacraments.

‘I finished something,’ Michael said, scrubbing. ‘And I can’t stop wanting the next page.’

R. dried a plate with a tea towel that had seen better graphics. ‘That’s writers. And addicts. Overlap larger than either group admits. Also people who have just fallen in love with an idea. Ideas are terrible at setting boundaries.’

‘Not a sequel for ego,’ he said. ‘A bigger book because what I landed didn’t close the world—it opened it. If there’s no outside, then other people’s chapters aren’t optional downloadable content. They’re the same story in different typefaces.’

R. set the plate down carefully. China on wood: a small click of finality. ‘And my tradition says the prophetic chapter has a seal. Finality is not a lack of imagination. It’s a mercy against endless freelancers claiming heaven’s letterhead. I’ve watched freelancers. They always sound sure. Sure is cheap.’

There it was. Not a fight yet. A clean edge on the table between them.

Michael turned off the tap. Water noise died. The radio offered a song about motorways, which is to say about motion without arrival.

‘I hear that,’ he said. ‘I do. Seal as protection. Seal as completion. Seal as *stop inventing gods with your cleverness*—which, given my recent stationery habits, I probably need tattooed somewhere inconvenient.’

‘But,’ R. said, half-smiling.

‘But I bristle at final chapter if final chapter means *your curiosity is closed, sit down*. I want to keep writing. Not over your book. Beside it. Through my own life. Through science and shame and keyboards and brothers. If God is not a coward, why would God fear another honest page?’

R.’s eyes hardened—not with hate, with the steel of someone who has watched ‘honest pages’ become excuses for contempt. She folded the tea towel once, twice, as if folding could keep the conversation from spilling.

‘Because people use *ongoing* as a weapon,’ she said. ‘They say the story continues and then write me out of adulthood. They say God still speaks and then the voice sounds remarkably like their politics. Final chapter, for me, is not anti-thought. It’s anti-capture. The Qur’an is not waiting for your monogram to complete it. It was not incomplete until you had a good week.’

Michael flinched because the shot was fair. Fair shots leave bruises that instruct.

‘I’m not completing you,’ he said. ‘I’m admitting I can’t treat my recognition as the last train. There’s a difference between *my* chapter ending in a joke I needed and *the universe* ending because I got a joke about fonts.’

R. leaned on the counter. ‘Say that again without the fonts.’

He breathed. Counted the breath like a man learning not to sprint into other people’s rooms. ‘I don’t need your final chapter to be wrong for my bigger book to be lived. And I don’t need my bigger book to annex your final chapter to feel real. Annexation is what empires do with maps. I’m trying not to be an empire in a cardigan.’

Silence. A neighbour’s television laughed through the wall on cue, as if the field had a laugh track and a sense of timing.

R. nodded once. ‘That’s the least stupid version you’ve said. Keep that version. Lose the version where seal equals small and ongoing equals mature. That’s spiritual colonialism with better coffee. I’ve sat through the mature people. They’re exhausting.’

‘Ouch.’

‘Deserved, if you felt it,’ she said, but softer. ‘Michael. I love that you think. I will not be your project of expansion. My faith is not the prequel to your both-and shop. I am not the missing chapter that makes your novel complete.’

He nodded, throat tight. ‘And I will not ask you to call what I saw prophecy. I will not call your seal a failure of imagination. Those are the red lines I’m writing on my own hands.’

‘Good. Then we can keep arguing without divorce. Divorce is what happens when one person needs to win the metaphysics more than keep the friend.’

Joshua’s key in the door—he still had one, brother privilege—preceded Joshua himself, bearing wine he probably shouldn’t have bought and an apology for lateness that was mostly sincere.

‘What did I miss?’ he asked, reading the air like weather.

‘Final chapter versus bigger book,’ R. said. ‘No blood. Some soap. Possible growth, pending inspection.’

Joshua looked at both of them with the pride of a man whose friends could fight clean. He set the wine down and did not open it yet; presence first. ‘Both true in different rooms,’ he said. ‘Seal for her integrity. Ongoing page for his refusal to stop loving the world. Neither gets a veto on the other’s conscience. Conscience is not a committee.’

‘Amen,’ R. said, surprising herself into a grin.

‘Amen,’ Michael said, and meant the both without using the brand as a flag.

They ate the cooled pasta. The salad was better than the sauce. The bread did its quiet work of making people stay. They did not resolve the metaphysics of history. History would not have attended the meeting anyway.

They resolved, for one kitchen evening, that friendship could hold a seal and a blank page on the same table without either becoming a weapon—which is a kind of scripture no one printed, and everyone present signed by staying for dessert.
