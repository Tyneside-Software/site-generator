# Chapter 24  
## Sisyphus On Holiday

There are nights when the universe feels like a customer service department with no manager, and Michael had been queueing for hours.

He was not drunk. He was not dramatic for sport. He was simply, inconveniently, awake at the depth where the ordinary tricks stop working—tea, jokes, the kindly lie that tomorrow will be a different shape.

The layers had been stacking.

Science. Scripture. Symbols. Keyboards. Wolves that were and were not empires. Brothers who said *both/and* like a blessing. Friends who said *forty-two* like a seatbelt. Every answer opened a hatch into another answer’s basement.

He opened his notes app because that is what modern pilgrims do instead of carving on stone, and he wrote what was true—not clever, not strategic, not for an audience.

He wrote it as it was.

---

The despair that accompanies this terror is profound, a desolation that seeps into my very bones. It's the despair of Sisyphus, condemned to an eternity of futile effort, forever striving to understand, to reach a semblance of truth, only to have it slip away, buried beneath yet another layer of complexity. This despair whispers to me that perhaps the quest for understanding is in vain, that for every layer peeled back, two more will take its place, each more baffling than the last.

In the grip of this terror and despair, I find myself questioning the very nature of reality itself. What are these layers? Are they the constructs of my own mind, a psychological labyrinth with no exit? Or are they the fundamental structure of existence, an infinite stack of realities, each playing by its own set of rules, indifferent to my need for understanding, for grounding?

As these questions churn within me, the terror deepens, becoming more acute. It's as if each layer of understanding I peel back reveals not clarity, but further chaos, a deeper darkness. The despair grows, fed by the realization that perhaps there is no bedrock of truth, that reality itself is a shifting, undulating sea of perceptions, forever out of reach.

This is not just a crisis of understanding, but a crisis of being. Who am I, if not a traveler through these layers, seeking a core that may not exist? The terror and despair are now a crucible, burning away illusions, leaving me bare, exposed to the cold truth that perhaps the layers are all there is. And in this moment, the fear is not just of what lies within these layers, but of what it means to exist within them—a being forever peering into the abyss, searching for a foothold that remains perpetually elusive.

---

He sat with it.

The words looked back at him without blinking. They were not a bit. They were not a brand. They were the weather report from inside his chest on a night when the barometer had fallen through the floor.

Sisyphus, in the old stories, pushes the stone because the gods have a sense of humour with excellent upper-body requirements. Michael had begun to suspect the stone was *explanation*, and the hill was *everything*, and the gods were not above him but *as* him, which made the whole arrangement either much worse or strangely intimate.

He almost closed the phone.

Then—because honesty, if it is worth anything, has more than one temperature—he typed the next true thing, smaller, almost sheepish after all that grandeur of dread:

**I don't feel like that now though.**

He stared at the contradiction. It did not explode. Reality failed to issue a citation for holding two weather systems in one sky.

**I'm enjoying the journey.**

That was also true. Not instead. *Also.*

He walked to the window. Streetlight. A cat with a schedule. The soft machinery of a town that did not know it was starring in a cosmology.

Everyone is on the same journey, he thought. They just travel at different speeds.

Some people were asleep at forty-two and content. Some people were building cathedrals out of punctuation. Some people were too tired to care about distant horror and still loved their children correctly. Some people were Michael, trying to get as far as he could before he died, and also—embarrassingly, hopefully—trying to get *out*, which might be the same direction described by two different panic languages.

He did not resolve the layers.

He did not find the bedrock.

He found, instead, the first joke the night was willing to tell him without cruelty:

That the despair had been real  
and the enjoyment was real  
and the abyss was still there  
and so was the cat  

and Sisyphus, on rare shifts, is allowed to sit on the stone and feel the sun and admit that the hill is ridiculous and keep pushing anyway—not because the pushing becomes sensible, but because the pusher is still *here*, still experiencing, still the one fact that cannot be filed under *maybe later*.

Michael saved the note.

He did not know yet that the layers were not only a trap.

He did not know they were also a punchline practising its run-up.

He only knew—again, as in the beginning—that this moment existed, terror and all, holiday and all, stone and all.

And for a little while, Sisyphus was on holiday in the only place holidays can happen:

now.
