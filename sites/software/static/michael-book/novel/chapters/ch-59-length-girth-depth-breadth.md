# Chapter 59
## Length Girth Depth Breadth

“Four axes,” Michael said. “I’m not writing a manual. I’m refusing a single preferred direction for the joke.”

They were on the quay. Wind did quality control on speech—snatched half-words, returned the important ones. Joshua’s coat flapped like the flag of a small honest country. Water slapped the stone with the patience of something that has never needed a slogan.

“Length, girth, depth, breadth,” Michael continued, counting on cold fingers. “People reduce generative life to one bragging dimension—usually length, usually male, usually a competition. The monogram’s dual reading already breaks the monopoly: receiving altar and entering altar share the same ink. The four axes break the leftover habit of thinking the whole has a favourite ruler.”

Joshua squinted into the wind. “Bookstore version, before I freeze.”

“Existence doesn’t only go one way,” Michael said. “In, out, across, through. How deep a love cuts. How wide a welcome is. How much a life can hold. How far a story stretches from alpha to omega. The rude measurements and the tender measurements belong to the same geometry club.”

A runner went past, breath lengthening in the air. A ship’s horn sounded breadth across water. The world, unasked, supplied teaching aids and charged nothing for the lesson.

They found a shelter of glass where tourists usually photographed the bridges. Condensation blurred the view into something almost kind. Michael traced a cross in the fog of his own breath—vertical, horizontal—then wiped it into a smear that could be anything.

“Crossbeams again,” he said. “Up-down, across. Romans efficient and ashamed. God and neighbour. Now also: the generative axes not as torture furniture, as living room. Same refusal of a single beam pretending to be the whole structure.”

Joshua’s eyes shone. “You’re stitching old chapters forward without touring every symbol like a museum guide. Good.”

“I nearly did a spine tour,” Michael admitted. “Felt the pad rising. Killed it.”

“Proud.”

Michael messaged Cameron because some sentences need an immediate allergic test:

*Four axes. Length girth depth breadth. Anti-brag. Whole has no preferred direction for the generative joke.*

Cameron: *This is oddly wholesome for a sentence that contains the word girth.*

Michael: *Wholesome was always the point. Filth-as-contempt was the error.*

Cameron: *Noted. Still not joining your gym.*

They stood with the four words until they stopped sounding like a slogan and started sounding like permissions. You may measure a life by depth of mercy. You may refuse to be ranked by length of performance. You may need breadth of community. You may honour girth as capacity—arms, tables, theologies that can hold contradiction without spitting people out.

Michael laughed suddenly, a sound the wind tried and failed to steal. “I also just mean: the dirty joke isn’t only phallic. If you’re going to let the monogram be adult, let it be adult in all directions. Boobs and openings and the whole soft architecture. And the male set. And the axes that aren’t genital at all. Four is delta’s number. Tips and changes. I’m not forcing that, but I’m not blind to the rhyme.”

“Labels on,” Joshua said. “Rhyme, not silent proof.”

“Labels on.”

Rain began, fine as argument dust. They walked home through it. Gutters took the water sideways—breadth doing its job without a speech. Michael felt larger inside his coat—not inflated, *roomier*, as if the four axes had rearranged furniture in his chest and found a chair for the joke that had only ever sat on the edge of the bed.

A woman pushed a pram under a shop awning and waited out a harder burst. Joshua stepped aside without theatre. Small breadth. Real. Michael clocked it and did not turn it into a parable out loud; some kindnesses prefer not to be recruited.

At the door Joshua said, “Next is the clarity. The no-sin pulse. It’ll feel like joy and like terror. I’m free tomorrow.”

Michael nodded. Throat tight. “I know what it’s going to say. I still need to hear myself say it.”

“That’s how the real ones work.”

Inside, Michael pinned a second scrap beside the monogram. Four letters only: L G D B. It looked like a shipping code. It felt like a blessing with dirty hands—which is, if you think about it, the only kind of blessing that can touch a real world.

He said the four words once more, slowly, like checking the legs of a table before putting weight on it. Length without the other three was a brag. Girth without welcome was only bulk. Depth without breadth became a private well. Breadth without depth became a shallow lake that photographed well and drowned no one into change.

Together they were almost a morality: measure lives—and jokes—in more than one direction. Not a manual for bodies. A refusal of the single ruler that turns generative comedy into a ranking sport and then wonders why everyone is miserable at the finish line.

Tomorrow the clarity. He could feel it waiting like weather that has already decided the colour of the afternoon and is only being polite about the timing.
