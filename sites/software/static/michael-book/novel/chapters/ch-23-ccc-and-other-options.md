# Chapter 23
## CCC And Other Options

Monday night of systems week, Joshua's flat smelled of coffee and dry-wipe ink.

The whiteboard was already half full when Michael arrived—arrows, question marks, a dinosaur sketched with more enthusiasm than accuracy. Joshua stood with a blue marker like a conductor who had lost the orchestra and decided to draw one instead.

'Rules,' Joshua said. 'No crowning. No sneering. Options only. We are cataloguing maybes that keep honest people awake.'

Michael hung his coat. 'CCC?'

'Conformal cyclic cosmology, among friends who like syllables.' Joshua tapped a circle that bit its own tail. 'Universes that go quiet and deep and then, by a kind of mathematical kindness, become the seed of the next. Roger Penrose's neighbourhood. Not our dogma. An option on the shelf.'

Michael read other headings in black:

*Heat death garden party*  
*Bounce*  
*Simulation (handle with comedy)*  
*One long now with costumes*  
*We don't know and still have to love our kids*

'That's my favourite,' Michael said, pointing at the last.

'Mine too. It stays even if a better equation arrives.'

They worked for an hour the way brothers work when the stakes are real and the tone must not become a cult. Joshua added a note about previous aeons leaving ghosts in the maths. Michael added *dinosaurs as punchline, not proof*. They drew a little hotel infinity in the corner as a mascot. They left a box empty on purpose and labelled it *something kinder than our current language*.

'Why do this?' Michael asked eventually. 'Week one was cold floors and no pure nothing. Why the carnival?'

'Because systems week ends in layers if you're not careful. Brains, fields, numbers, levels—each one opens a hatch. People either grab the first hatch and call it home, or they fall through all of them and call it despair. I want a third way: look at the hatches. Wave. Don't live in the fall.'

Michael thought of ants, vacuum invoices, imaginary bills that paid for real light. 'And CCC?'

'A beautiful maybe. Endings can be beginnings without a magician outside the show. It rhymes with eternal-no-edge without forcing a single myth to do all the work. Keep it as maybe. Maybes are oxygen. Dogmas are closed windows.'

Michael uncapped a red marker: *Both/and applies to cosmologies too—useful stories, not exclusive landlords.*

Joshua nodded. 'Cameron would hate the mess. He'd want one shelf, labelled forty-two, lights out at a decent hour.'

'We'll need him,' Michael said, surprising himself. 'Sandpaper. Soon.'

'After the night that is coming,' Joshua said, and looked as if he wished he had not said it like weather.

Michael felt it then—the approach of something heavier than whiteboards. Not superstition. Pattern recognition. When you open enough hatches in one week, sleep becomes a negotiation.

They erased nothing. They photographed the board. Joshua wrote at the bottom, large:

**NOT KNOWING IS NOT NOTHING.**

Michael added underneath:

**OPTIONS ARE NOT ORDERS.**

They ordered a curry. Ordinary food under extraordinary diagrams. The dinosaur sketch watched them eat with prehistoric neutrality.

'If I spiral tonight,' Michael said, only half joking, 'remind me of the kids line.'

'I'll remind you of the curry first. Horizontal beam. Then the kids line. Then that enjoyment is allowed even when the layers won't sit still.'

Michael almost asked what Joshua foresaw. He did not. Some nights you should not name before they arrive; naming can become invitation.

At the door Joshua squeezed his shoulder. 'You've done good work this week. Consciousness not late. Surprise as contact. Wave and point. Vacuum. Zero. Imaginary. Emergence. Now maybes with manners. Enough bricks for one man before sleep.'

'Allegedly,' Michael said.

'Allegedly.'

Walking home under a sky full of unpaid stellar overtime, Michael felt the options flutter like cards. Previous universes. Future silences. Present dishes in the sink. A drunk couple argued about whether they were late or early for a party that might not exist; their voices rose and fell like a small cosmology. He washed two plates when he got in—defiance against pure abstraction—and left the curry container to soak because even apocalypses of meaning allowed logistics.

Against his better judgement he scrolled a late feed of confident strangers: this universe is a sim; a test; a joke; a machine that hates you. Each claim wore the costume of finality. None of them washed a plate. He put the phone face down.

He did not know he would need the whiteboard sentence later, in the small hours, when layers stacked like accusations and Sisyphus clocked in without holiday pay. He did not know the despair would be real and the enjoyment would also be real, and that holding both would feel like treason to every either/or he had ever hired for comfort.

He only knew the week had brought him to the edge of a long night—

—and that the night, like everything else, would still have to happen *as experience*, with no pure nothing to resign into, no outside door marked EXIT FROM BEING, only the hard mercy of still being here while the maybes argued.

He made tea.

He did not yet drink it.

The dark beyond the window was full of maybes, and one of them was sleep—and one of them, quiet as a held breath, was the morning after, when friends would speak of different speeds and ordinary numbers, if he could only get through the hill tonight.
