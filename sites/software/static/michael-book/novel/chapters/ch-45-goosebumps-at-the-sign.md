# Chapter 45
## Goosebumps At The Sign

The sign was not mystical.

It was a road sign with a dent, a bus timetable bleached by sun, and—taped crookedly to a lamppost—a handwritten notice about a lost cat named Mabel who liked Dreamies and had a notched ear. The tape had already begun to peel. Someone’s hope was losing a small argument with wind. A phone number sat soft with old rain, ink feathered so that calling would require faith as well as a thumb.

Michael stopped because his body stopped. The hairs on his arms rose as if a small weather system had formed under his skin.

Goosebumps.

He waited for a voice. None came. The street continued being a street: someone dragging a suitcase on a broken wheel; a gull committing tax fraud on a chip wrapper; the smell of rain that had not yet decided to arrive. A van indicated left and then went right with the moral confidence of local traffic.

Still the skin insisted. Data without a spreadsheet.

He photographed nothing. He stood with the sensation the way you stand with a song you cannot name—curious, slightly exposed, unwilling to recruit strangers into your private cinema. A woman with a pushchair glanced at him, then at the poster, then away. She had her own weather. Michael stepped aside so the pushchair could pass, which was manners, not epiphany, and still felt like belonging to the same map as the missing cat.

He walked home the long way, arms still faintly electric. Pavement, the cut-through by the takeaway, the stretch where the trees pretend they own the estate. He remembered other skin-moments without forcing them into a system: cold floor on day one; the airless relief of no outside left; a hymn in a supermarket between pasta sauces. None of them had issued bylaws. All of them had been real as weather—present, temporary, not requiring a creed to have happened.

At the kitchen table he tried an experiment he would later be slightly ashamed of and also glad about. He set his forearms on the wood. He thought deliberately of the landfill that failed, of sin-as-elsewhere cracking, of the ordinary number and the plank. Mild interest. A faint professional tingle. Then he thought of nothing in particular and watched sunlight move across the floorboards like a slow blessing with no paperwork.

The goosebumps did not return on command.

That was useful. Sacred skin is a poor servant of the ego. It comes when it comes, like cats and grace and good jokes. You can invite it. You cannot hire it as a notary. Michael rubbed his arms anyway, half hoping, half checking the meter, and laughed at himself when nothing answered.

Joshua rang while Michael was still staring at the floorboards.

“You went quiet,” Joshua said. “I can hear you thinking through the ether, which is a ridiculous thing to say and also true.”

“Goosebumps at a lamppost,” Michael said. “Lost cat poster. No voice. Just skin.”

“Somatic. File as contact, not command. Body often arrives before story.”

“I want to make it mean the chain is true.” Michael heard the hunger and corrected mid-flight. “I mean—I notice I want that. Which is already half the problem.”

“Of course you do. That’s the hungry bit.” Joshua’s voice softened. “Let the goosebumps be goosebumps first. Meaning can apply for a visa later. Hydrate. Don’t found a church on histamine—Cameron’s line, and he’s right.”

After the call Michael poured water and actually drank it. He wrote carefully, as if the pen might overclaim:

**Goosebumps are data.**  
**Data is not automatically doctrine.**  
**The body can say yes without writing a creed.**

Later, at the corner shop, he saw another poster—same handwriting, Mabel still missing, the number even softer. He felt a smaller echo of the earlier charge, almost kindness rather than awe. Someone loved a cat enough to tape paper to metal in a windy city. That was already meaning enough for one afternoon. He bought milk he did not strictly need so the trip would have a receipt and not only a mood.

Evening, he stood under his own shower and felt ordinary water do ordinary work. No epiphany. Clean skin. The memory of lamppost weather remained like a stamp on an envelope already sent. He did not need the goosebumps to prove the closed map. The closed map had arrived with plates and panic. The goosebumps were something else: the field touching the instrument that measures it from inside, briefly, without a demand that he found a ministry.

Joshua rang once more, softer. “Still okay?”

“Yes,” Michael said. “Slightly ridiculous. Slightly grateful. Trying not to turn arm-hair into a proof.”

“Good man. Proofs that need goosebumps go missing when you have a cold.”

Michael laughed. “There’s a sentence for the back cover.”

“Don’t you dare.”

After the call he left a saucer of water in the garden for any cat that was not Mabel and might still be thirsty. The saucer tilted on a brick until he levelled it with a second brick and a patience he usually reserved for code. It was not magic. It was manners toward a world that kept posting notices about love in the rain.

He refused a forced moral in the notebook. Tomorrow he might walk past a doorway that was only a doorway until he counted it otherwise. Tonight was arm-hair weather and a missing cat and a city that taped its hopes to metal.

When he slept, he dreamed no symbols.

He dreamed of a notched ear turning a corner into a street that knew how to wait, and of tape that held a little longer than the wind deserved.
