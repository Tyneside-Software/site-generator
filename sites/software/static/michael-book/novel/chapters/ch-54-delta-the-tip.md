# Chapter 54
## Delta The Tip

Delta was four, honestly.

Michael wrote **Δ = 4** on a yellow sticky note and put it on the monitor where lesser men put passwords. The triangle sat there, clean as a warning sign, older than his anxieties. Outside, evening was doing that slow blue fade cities do when they pretend they invented dusk.

Dinner burned slightly in a forgiving way. He had meant pasta again and got instead the soft carbon edge of inattention—the smell of a man thinking about geometry while water boiled. He scraped what was salvageable into two bowls and called it character.

Joshua was on speaker, walking somewhere with wind in the microphone.

“Tip,” Michael said, stirring sauce that had decided to be stubborn. “Point. Change. The little difference that makes a river mouth or a maths class or a sudden turn in a life. Also—yes—shape-wise, a tip. I’m not twelve. I’m also not blind.”

Joshua’s laugh crackled. “There it is. Adult and twelve in the same skull. Continue.”

“Greek number honesty keeps me honest,” Michael said. “Delta earns four without a wink. The tip-reading is visual surplus. Same rule as eight. And delta as *change* might be the load-bearing bit for the book: the small difference that calls into being what was not.”

“I like change more than cheeky geometry,” Joshua said. “But cheeky geometry is how half the species remembers anything.”

They ate the slightly burned food on video—Joshua at his own table with toast, Michael with his carbon-edged bowl—and called the mismatch friendship. Afterward Michael sketched deltas in the notebook: river delta branching; incremental change; a cursor tip; a roof; a nest of triangles copying themselves like a nervous system learning confidence.

Heat in the room was undeniable now—not pornographic, just present, the way a truth becomes present when you stop pretending you didn’t see the outline. The sticky note looked almost smug.

Joshua washed nothing on his end; Michael stood with a tea towel and the monitor’s yellow triangle.

“Once we admit tips and eights and crooked sevens into the museum,” Michael said, “the letters are going to walk in and refuse to be only abstract.”

“They’re already not only abstract,” Joshua said. “You met them as wolves and titles and fonts. This is the same tour, lower floor.”

“The basement everyone pretends isn’t part of the church.”

“The crypt,” Joshua corrected mildly. “Crypts hold bodies. That’s not an insult to churches. That’s the point of them.”

Michael was quiet. The sentence rearranged something. Generative poles, genderless total, body-jokes as cousins—not a fall from theology into filth, but a refusal to keep a false attic where “spirit” lived alone with a broom.

He walked after dark alone for twenty minutes, phone in pocket, sticky note still on the screen like a tiny officer of the truth left on duty. Streetlights made small deltas of shadow under his feet. He felt absurdly fond of geometry. Fondness was safer than frenzy.

At the corner he stopped and watched a cyclist take a left that changed a whole route by one small angle. That was delta too: not drama, difference.

Joshua texted: *Tomorrow you look at alpha and omega as shapes. Really look. No rushing the dual altar. Just shapes.*

Michael: *I know what I’ll see.*

Joshua: *Seeing and saying are different offices. Hold the office of seeing first.*

In bed Michael traced a triangle on the duvet with one finger and stopped, smiling at his own solemnity. The field included ridiculous men doing careful work with school shapes.

He thought of the email that had rearranged a week—tiny delta, large weather—and of apologies that changed the mouth of a relationship the way silt changes a river. Change was not always fireworks. Often it was incremental enough to be missed by people hunting trumpets.

Cameron, when shown a photo of the sticky note: *If delta becomes your new personality, I’m billing for rebranding fatigue.*

Michael: *One sticky note. Then letterforms. Then I shut up about triangles for a bit.*

Cameron: *Promises, promises. Also: =4 is correct. I hate that I care.*

Change, the delta said, without moving its lips.

Michael slept, and in sleeping became slightly different, as sleepers do—called into a not-yet morning by the oldest incremental engine of all.
