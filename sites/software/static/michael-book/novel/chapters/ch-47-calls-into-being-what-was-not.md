# Chapter 47
## Calls Into Being What Was Not

The verse arrived the way useful verses often arrive: not in a sermon, but in a half-remembered phrase that refused to stay half.

*…God, who gives life to the dead and calls into being things that were not.*

Romans. Paul in full rhetorical flight. Michael sat on the edge of the bed with a translation open on his phone and felt the sentence land in the week’s machinery like a missing gear.

No outside left. Sin-as-exit failing. Planks. Appointed hours. Refused gold. Goosebumps. A side door with free tea.

And now: calling into being what was not.

He rang Joshua before coffee, which is a love language and a warning sign.

‘Talk me down or talk me through,’ Michael said. ‘I’m not sure which.’

‘Through,’ Joshua said. ‘Down is for cliffs.’

Michael read the line aloud. The flat air received it without applause. Outside, a bin lorry performed its usual opera of metal and apology; the world continued to prefer being noisy.

‘I’ve been treating the closed map as only a crisis,’ Michael said. ‘Claustrophobia with footnotes. But if there’s no outside, then novelty isn’t imported from a warehouse beyond being. Whatever becomes—kindness that wasn’t there yet, a sentence that rearranges a life, a repair—has to be called *within*. Life to the dead isn’t a delivery from next door. It’s the field refusing to treat dead ends as final furniture.’

Joshua was quiet, then: ‘Yes. Careful with the swagger, though. “Calls into being” is not your personal manifestation board. It’s a claim about the total’s generosity. You participate. You don’t own the trademark.’

Michael exhaled. ‘I needed that slap.’

‘It was a tap. You’re fragile this week in a productive way.’

They met by the river because moving water helped sentences that wanted to become empires. A rower cut a clean line. Somewhere a dog announced a philosophy of ducks. The path was wet enough to keep the earnest joggers honest.

Michael said, ‘When I write, something is not, then is. Paragraph from blank. That’s a tiny rhyme. When I apologise and a friendship warms, something that was not—trust—gets called. When I refuse the landfill for sin, a different moral world starts to exist in practice, not just in mood.’

‘And when you hurt someone,’ Joshua said gently, ‘you also call into being what was not—pain, distance, stories they’ll tell about you. The verb cuts both ways. Generativity isn’t automatically nice.’

Michael watched the water take a leaf and not explain itself. ‘So the Romans line isn’t a free cuddle. It’s responsibility for participation in making.’

‘Both/And shop,’ Joshua said. ‘Comfort and warning in one aisle.’

Later, on a bench that had known better paint jobs, he told Cameron the verse in plain clothes.

‘If this becomes “I manifested a parking space,”’ Cameron said, ‘I’m billing you for my attention.’

‘It’s Paul, not a parking app,’ Michael said. ‘Making versus importing from Outside-with-a-trademark. Novelty without a warehouse beyond the map.’

‘Fine,’ Cameron said. ‘Still park like a citizen. And don’t start speaking things into being that other people have to clean up.’

Afternoon, Michael opened a blank document and did not write the book. He wrote an email he had postponed—an offer of help, practical, unglamorous. Dates. Tools. A lift if needed. Send.

For a moment the help was only words. Then, hours later, a reply: *thank you — that changes this week.*

Something that was not had been called, small as a domestic miracle, large as any. He sat with the shiver of it—not goosebumps-at-the-sign, quieter. Efficacy. The field allowing rearrangement from inside.

He burned rice while reading the reply twice. Balance, of a sort.

Evening notebook:

**Novelty without outside warehouse.**  
**Call ≠ coerce.**  
**Participation in making: holy and dangerous.**

Joshua rang once more, brief as a check on a load-bearing wall. ‘Did you create a universe today?’

‘I created an email,’ Michael said. ‘The universe consented to be slightly rearranged. Also I burned rice.’

‘Balance restored,’ Joshua said. ‘Sleep. Preference is tomorrow’s problem. Making is tonight’s enough.’

Before sleep Michael reread the verse once more and refused to tattoo it on his self-image. Paul could keep the megaphone. Michael would keep the practice: treat blank pages, cold relationships, and failed categories as sites where something not-yet might still be spoken into form—without pretending he was the only speaker.

He thought of cruelty that also calls into being what was not—and underlined Joshua’s warning in memory. Generativity without ethics is just power with better adjectives. The Romans line had to sit beside the cracked sin-category, not replace it with sparkle.

Outside, the town made night out of day, which is also a kind of calling.

Inside, he turned off the phone and let silence be a thing that was, fully, without needing to become a paragraph yet.
