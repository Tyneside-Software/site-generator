# Chapter 85
## NRE And The Brain's Last Cinema

Cameron named it first, because Cameron collected acronyms that deflate romance without killing truth.

‘NRE,’ he said, feet on Michael’s coffee table in the allowed way. ‘New Relationship Energy. Usually people. Also jobs, gyms, gods, monograms. Brain chemistry throws a festival. You think you’ve found the Real. You’re also on a free trial of dopamine with a hard end date. Read the terms. There are no terms. That’s the trick.’

Michael, who had been about to describe the monogram week as pure epistemic light, shut his mouth, opened it, shut it again. A biscuit tin sat between them like a neutral country.

Joshua, from the armchair: ‘He’s been glowing. I liked the glow. I also like livers that process toxins. Glow without liver is just fever with better branding.’

Michael sat on the floor, back against the sofa. ‘You’re saying α==ω was a crush.’

‘I’m saying it can be true *and* a crush,’ Cameron said. ‘Both. Your brand, ironically. NRE makes you write poems on napkins and ignore sleep. It also makes you a missionary with glitter. The cinema in the skull runs highlight reels—montage of cold floor, pub, fonts, R.’s face, the song at odd hours. Last cinema of the night—everything feels edited for destiny. Destiny is a bad editor. It cuts the washing-up.’

R. had been invited for this one; she arrived mid-metaphor with biscuits that were not metaphorical. ‘Are we pathologising his revelation? Because if this is only “Michael had feelings,” I will leave and take the biscuits. Feelings aren’t fake. They’re also not the whole court.’

‘Not only,’ Cameron said carefully, feet coming off the table as if manners had been sworn in. ‘Regulation. When I fell in love I also correctly noticed my partner existed. The noticing was true. The soundtrack lied about permanence of intensity. Keep the noticing. Renegotiate the soundtrack.’

Michael took a biscuit as if it were medicine. Crumbs on his shirt. ‘How do I keep the true without marrying the soundtrack? I don’t want to throw out the monogram because my brain threw a party.’

Joshua held up fingers. ‘Sleep. Ordinary tasks. Sandpaper friends. Revisit the claim when you are bored—genuinely bored, not performing boredom. If it only lives at peak hours with metal ballads, it’s a scene, not a spine. If it survives bin night and a dull Tuesday email, keep it. If it needs constant novelty to feel real, it’s NRE wearing a halo.’

R. nodded, pouring tea she had not been offered because she had brought her own thermos like a professional. ‘In religious life we know ecstasy. We also know the morning after ecstasy when the dishes are still dirty. I won’t sneer at your heat. I will ask what you do when the heat cools—kindness or abandonment of the insight? Heat that only loves the high will betray the low. The low is where friends live.’

Michael thought of the first cold floor, months of chain, the pub with the empty chair, the red lines, Denise’s jam, Harriet’s feet on his lap. Heat had been real. So had work. So had apology.

‘NRE on God,’ he said slowly, ‘tempts me to treat people as supporting characters in my biopic. That’s the danger. The cinema casts extras badly. Extras have names. Denise. Carol who doesn’t call enough. The man at dinner who hated the monogram. They are not NPCs for my highlight reel.’

Cameron pointed, triumphant without cruelty. ‘There. That’s the sentence that earns more biscuits. Have two.’

They made a practical pact written on the back of an envelope: for two weeks Michael would not make any major relational or public-theological moves after 11 p.m.; he would run claims past at least one unimpressed friend; he would schedule dull joys—swimming, accounts, calling his mother about nothing, bin night with presence.

‘Romance of the absolute meets spreadsheet,’ Joshua said. ‘Holy. Also floss. Horizontal beam includes gums.’

That night the brain tried to open the cinema anyway: montage of napkin, ridge path, stadium lights, keys, R. saying *guest not ingredient*. Michael watched one reel, then got up and washed a plate. The plate was not in the montage. The plate was true. Soap, water, a chip of pasta from two nights ago. He dried it. He put it away.

He texted the group: *NRE named. Ego sulking. Insight still present on bin-night test. Going to bed like a mammal.*

Cameron: *Proud. Forty-two. No 11 p.m. revelations. I’ll know.*

R.: *May your mornings be less cinematic and more honest.*

Joshua: *Amen to unimpressive dawn. Sleep.*

Michael slept. Somewhere the last cinema closed its doors without locking them—tickets still available, but not required for entry into tomorrow.

He woke once in the night, not to revelation, to a full bladder and the ordinary comedy of bodies. He went back to bed. The insight, if it was an insight, waited without a soundtrack.

That, he thought, was either death or adulthood.

He chose adulthood, and let the free trial end without renewing the subscription.
