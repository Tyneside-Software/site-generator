# Chapter 98
## Almost Morning

The sky went from black to a colour that insisted it was not black anymore, and Michael was awake for it without having planned a vigil.

Almost morning. The house ticked. A pipe somewhere made a remark in the language of houses that have opinions about temperature. Harriet breathed the slow rhythm of people who have decided sleep is not a moral failure. On the chair by the door, his jacket held the wallet like a pocketed library: monogram napkin, bigger-book napkin, a receipt from soup night, a loyalty card for coffee he kept forgetting to stamp.

He stood at the window with tea and watched the street become itself. Bins at slight angles, as if they had argued and settled. A fox with a freelance career crossed the road without asking for a blurb. The first bus arrived with its cargo of early duty—people whose faces had not yet put on the day, only the coat. A streetlight hesitated, then admitted the sky was winning, and went out with the dignity of a minor official clocking off.

Almost morning was a tense that suited him. Not the climax of chapter sixty-five—napkin, pub, final line spoken into a room that had not asked for a sermon. Not yet the full daylight of toast and the reprise he could feel waiting like a train three stops away. The in-between: recognition sharing a body with fatigue, unpaid emails, desire, love that snored lightly in the next room, a hip that still complained if he pretended he was made of ideas.

He sipped tea gone lukewarm and did not mind. Lukewarm was honest. Joshua would still be asleep. Cameron would call early hours a personal attack and mean it. R. might already be awake in her own rhythm; Michael did not narrate that into his private film. He sent no texts. Almost morning can be ruined by performing it for an audience of phones.

A delivery van reversed with the same beep as on the first morning after recognition. Continuity as comedy. The universe, if it had a sense of humour—and Michael still thought it did—was not above using health-and-safety audio as a punchline.

He took the wallet from the jacket, removed the monogram napkin, and looked at it without electricity. α==ω. Dual joke. Generative claim. Still true. Also: paper, fold-worn, a bit ridiculous, survivable without empire and without a camera. He smoothed one corner that wanted to curl, the way paper does when it has been loved too hard and carried too long.

‘Almost morning,’ he told the glass. ‘We’re still here.’

The glass did not argue. Experience existed. The field had no outside. The neighbours would need kindness that was not a diagram. Denise’s boiler would fail again someday. He would go again if he could, jam or no jam, photo or no photo.

He put the napkin back. The wallet closed with a soft sound like a small book deciding not to make a scene.

In the kitchen he rinsed the mug and left it on the draining board—draft theology in crockery form. He sliced bread for toast and set the dial where it would not burn if he paid attention. Paying attention was the whole religion in work clothes: not the absence of God, not the presence of a press release, just the refusal to outsource the next hour to symbols while the bread blackened.

Outside, the fox vanished into a hedge with the professionalism of creatures who do not need last lines. A cyclist went past with a light slightly too bright, apologising to the dark by dazzling it. Michael watched until the street was only a street again.

He sat on the sofa and closed his eyes for twenty minutes of not-sleep, a soft buffer between night and day. Almost morning held him like a waiting room that was also the appointment. He did not inventory the bigger book. Inventory was yesterday’s temptation. Today’s work was simpler and harder: stay in the body that had to make coffee for two, say something unimpressive—*sleep okay?*—and mean it when Harriet’s alarm rang.

Her phone would chirp in an hour. He would not wake her early to share the sky. Shared sky is better when both people choose it. He had learned that the expensive way, with intensity seasons and the look she got when he treated dawn like a product launch.

A bird attempted a song and failed, tried again, succeeded in a thin way that still counted. Michael opened his eyes. The room was the room: sofa, chair, jacket, the faint smell of last night’s washing-up liquid. No vision. No metal ballad. No need.

He stood, stretched the hip, and put the kettle on for the second time—not as ritual, as thirst. When the water boiled he made coffee strong enough to survive conversation and left Harriet’s mug waiting beside the pot like a promise that did not require Greek.

Almost morning ended the way good thresholds do: without a drumroll. Light filled the kitchen corner by corner. Somewhere a bus took the early duty people toward their lives. Michael buttered nothing yet. He waited for the alarm, for the unimpressive question, for the day that would not ask him to caption it—

—and when Harriet’s phone finally sang, he was already pouring, already present, already almost ready to be fully here without needing the night to clap.
