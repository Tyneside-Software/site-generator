# Chapter 57
## The Other Reading

Morning. Same napkin. Different light.

The brewery logo still had opinions about hops. The biro lines still said α==ω, double equals like a programmer’s piety and a joke that had finally sat down at the table. M. turned the paper forty-five degrees, then back, then held it at arm’s length like a painting in a shop he could not afford.

J. sat opposite with coffee and the expression of a midwife who has seen stranger births.

“Say it plain,” J. said. “Bookstore plain. No cowardice, no swagger.”

M.’s face burned. He did it anyway.

“One reading of the monogram—in the fonts we actually live in—is cock and balls. Shaft, pair, the whole adolescent breakthrough every culture rediscovers and pretends is original. Alpha’s open body, omega’s twin lobes, the equals as a kind of insistence. It’s funny because it’s true-as-shape. It’s dangerous because people will think the joke *replaces* the span claim.”

J. didn’t flinch. “Other reading.”

“Same ink,” M. said. “Flip the emphasis—omega’s soft double curve as breasts in the crude glyph dialect humans use on steamy windows and notebooks. Alpha as open triangle, yonic geometry, the receiving altar. Same letters. Same monogram. Not a competing logo. A second office of seeing.”

Silence held for a beat. Then J. laughed—full, fond, relieved. “There. Both altars. I was waiting for you to stop protecting God from genitals.”

“I’m still blushing.”

“Blushing is appropriate. Exhibitionism isn’t required. Precision is.”

M. stood and paced the small kitchen. The fridge hummed like a bored monk. Toast crumbs from yesterday still occupied the counter with the confidence of squatters.

“This is the bit where someone says I’m reducing theology to smut.”

“And someone else says you’re baptising smut to look clever,” J. said. “Both objections assume the sacred and the generative body are two estates with a fence. You’ve spent forty chapters removing fences. This is only the removal becoming visible in a drawing.”

M. stopped. The sentence clicked like a seatbelt. “Ontology, not random filth.”

“Ontology,” J. agreed. “If the whole can’t be split, the name of the whole will eventually look like how wholes continue—entering and receiving, making and holding, the comic equipment of continuation. The dual reading is the grammar showing up as graffiti. Graffiti can be true.”

M. sent C. three lines, because sandpaper is a sacrament when applied early:

*Other reading spoken. Cock/balls and boobs/yonic open. Same monogram. Ontology, not filth-for-hire.*

The reply took long enough that M. imagined sunglasses being put on with ceremony.

*I can still see. Damn you. It’s coherent. I’m annoyed that it’s coherent.*

*Motte-and-bailey check?* M. sent.

*Motte: shapes rhyme with generative bodies; sacred span still primary. Bailey risk: my doodle proves metaphysics. Stay on motte in public. Bailey is for novels that know they’re novels.*

*Novel knows. Staying honest.*

A pause. Then: *…I’m proud of you in a way that will never be public.*

They went for a walk because kitchens can only hold so much revelation per cubic metre. In the park the spaniel from the wolf weeks was still winning at being a dog. A teenager spray-painted nothing more ambitious than a tag on a bin. Humanity continued its ancient project of making marks without filling out a theology form first.

M. said, “I don’t want a church of the monogram.”

“Good,” J. said. “Want a life that can laugh when the total shows up wearing a body-joke without ceasing to be the total.”

“Both altars,” M. repeated. “Not a competition. Concatenation.”

“Not either/or,” J. said. “That’s tomorrow’s title, isn’t it?”

“Yes.”

They stopped under a tree that had no opinion about Greek. A leaf fell with the solemnity of a prop. M. felt the last of a false piety fall off him like a wet coat—the piety that thought God needed protection from the way babies are made and jokes are drawn. What remained was not permission to be cruel or crude *at* people. What remained was refusal to castrate the divine to keep a drawing-room calm.

Back home he pinned a clean copy of the monogram above the desk. The pin went in crooked; he left it crooked. Perfection would have been a different religion.

He stood with his fingers still smelling faintly of metal and felt the old either/or try one last recruitment speech: pick the holy reading for the bookshop window, hide the body-reading for private boys’ jokes. He declined the speech. The whole point of the closed map was that the window and the joke lived in the same existence.

Then he made toast, burned it slightly, ate it anyway.

Ontology survived.

So did the blush.

Both, of course—both.
