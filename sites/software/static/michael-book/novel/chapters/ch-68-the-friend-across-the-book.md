# Chapter 68
## The Friend Across The Book

They met at the café that never quite decided whether it was Turkish, Italian, or a tax-efficient dream of both.

R. was already there—coat over the chair, phone face-down, a small modern piety. She waved him over with the ease of years: same table near the window if available, same order nearly, same refusal to pretend friendship required identical metaphysics.

‘You look,’ she said, studying him, ‘like a man who has either fallen in love or finished a dangerous book.’

‘Both,’ Michael said, then held up a hand. ‘Not romantic. Ontological. Don’t leave.’

She laughed, and the laugh did more theology than most sermons. ‘Sit. I’ve ordered you the thing you always pretend you won’t finish.’

He sat. Across the table, between their mugs, lay the invisible book neither owned alone: the shared world, the city, people arguing about God while still passing the sugar.

‘How’s work?’ he asked, because the ordinary is not small talk when you mean it.

She told him. He listened. A colleague’s drama, a parent’s appointment, a joke about traffic that was also true. Only after the second sip did she tilt her head.

‘And you? Joshua said you and he had a… morning.’

‘Joshua texts you?’

‘Joshua texts everyone when he thinks mercy requires a witness,’ R. said dryly. ‘He said you landed something. He didn’t explain. He respects privacy almost as much as he respects amens.’

Michael turned the mug. Steam rose and vanished—honest work for steam. ‘I can outline without recruiting you. That’s the deal I made with myself on the walk here.’

‘Good deal. Outline, not altar call.’

So he gave her the clean version: that experience exists; that nothing is not an outside; that he had found a shape which made him less cruel rather than more. He left out the parts that would sound like winning. He included the part where Cameron still went to bed, because sleep is a kind of peer review.

R. listened the way she always had—eyes steady, mouth ready to object, no fear of silence. A bus hissed at the stop outside. A child pressed a nose to the café glass and fogged a temporary moon.

‘That’s a lot of week,’ she said at last. ‘I’m glad you found a shape that doesn’t make you sharp at the edges. Some shapes do. I’ve met them. I’ve nearly become them when tired.’

‘I don’t want yours as a prop,’ Michael said. The sentence had been rehearsed and still shook. ‘When we talk faith, I want you as the friend across the book. Not a chapter I write you into. Not evidence. Not the Muslim Friend Who Proves My Field Is Wide.’

R. exhaled, something tight leaving her shoulders. ‘Then we can talk. I’ve had men explain my own book to me with diagrams. I’ve had women do it with pity. Friendship is rarer than both. Friendship means I get to remain opaque where I choose.’

‘Opaque is allowed,’ he said. ‘I’m still learning that opacity is not a bug.’

She smiled sideways. ‘Scripture Reasoning next week? The group at the centre. Not a debate club. Parallel reading. Jews, Christians, Muslims, the occasional brave atheist who thinks hospitality is a method.’

Michael felt the old pattern-itch—the urge to map, to make the room useful to his recognition—and sat on it until it behaved. ‘I’d like that. As guest. As learner. As the man who once nearly turned a bookshop window into a private museum and stopped.’

‘I remember that story,’ R. said. ‘You told it like a confession. Keep telling it that way. Confessions age better than trophies.’

He smiled. ‘Joshua’s coming if he can. Cameron said he’ll come once, leave early, and mock us kindly by text.’

‘Perfect quorum. Kind mockery is a ministry.’

She reached across and tapped the table between them—once, lightly, like sealing something without a stamp.

‘Across the book,’ she said. ‘Not through each other like paper. Paper gets torn. People shouldn’t.’

‘Across,’ Michael agreed.

They finished the drinks. They split the bill without theatre—no performance of generosity, just arithmetic and trust. On the pavement she adjusted her scarf against the wind and looked at him with the particular seriousness friends use when the joke can wait its turn.

‘One more thing,’ she said. ‘If whatever you landed makes you kinder to me, keep it. If it makes you hungrier to *solve* me, leave it at home. I am not a puzzle that completes your week. I am a person who will still be here when your week has moved on.’

‘Understood.’

‘We’ll see,’ R. said, but she said it smiling, which is how trust extends credit.

A cyclist rang a bell. The city continued its long argument with itself and did not require their agreement to keep the lights on.

Michael walked home lighter—not because the recognition had shrunk, but because it had met a person and failed to eat her. That failure felt like success of a rarer kind.

The bigger book did not begin with a conquest.

It began with a table, two mugs, and a friend who still got to be herself on the far page—unread where she chose, known where she offered, and never converted into proof that he had understood the universe.
