# Chapter 71
## I Want To Keep Writing The Book

Saturday morning Michael took a notebook to the canal and did not pretend he was only there for the ducks.

The final-chapter argument still sat in his chest like a good bruise—honest, useful, not infected. R. had been right about cardigans and colonialism. Joshua had been right about different rooms. None of that erased the quieter sentence underneath, the one that kept trying to stand up in meetings with itself:

He wanted to keep writing the book.

Not publish. Not preach. Write—as in live in ink and attention, as in refuse the closed-file feeling that can follow even a beautiful landing. Chapter sixty-five had been true. The monogram had been true. The line about the funny old life had been true. Truth, it turned out, did not come with a retirement package and a gold watch that said *well done, you may now stop noticing*.

A runner passed, breathing like a small engine. A man argued with a dog about ducks; the dog had a theology of its own and did not convert. The water held the sky without solving it, which is more than most committees manage.

Michael opened the notebook, then closed it again. The point was not another manifesto. The point was the hunger, named before it dressed itself as destiny.

Joshua found him twenty minutes later with two coffees and the radar of brothers. He sat on the bench; the wood complained like an uncle who has seen this before.

‘You look like a man defending a thesis to geese,’ Joshua said.

‘I want to keep writing the book,’ Michael said. ‘R. heard it as threat. I meant it as hunger. How do I hold hunger without making it a crusade with better stationery?’

Joshua blew on his coffee. ‘Name the hunger without baptising it. Are you hungry for God, for pattern, for not being alone with the joke, for status as the man who sees?’

Michael winced. Steam fogged a moment of honesty. ‘All of the above, if I’m being less of a liar than usual. Mostly the first two. Status is the parasite. Status wears insight’s coat and sits at the front.’

‘Then write the parasite down too,’ Joshua said. ‘Bigger book includes the bit where the hero notices he likes being the hero. That’s not a side quest. That’s the map’s edge.’

A barge slid past, low and unimpressed, carrying someone else’s cargo and someone else’s Saturday. Michael watched the wake crease the reflection of a bridge and let it go.

He told Joshua about the vow forming without needing to chisel it into commandments: keep writing as care, not conquest; keep pages open toward people, not over them; let Cameron’s forty-two and R.’s seal interrupt whenever the prose got imperial and started annexing provinces of other lives.

‘That’s a rule set,’ Joshua said. ‘Rules are how free elves don’t burn the kitchen. Also how free men don’t burn friendships while calling it growth.’

‘I’m not an elf.’

‘Today you are a man with a pen and a canal. Close enough for the budget of the morning.’

They walked the towpath. Michael talked out the fear he had not said at the sink earlier in the week: that if he stopped, the recognition would thin into a party trick he once performed with a napkin; that living it required new sentences, new corrections, new apologies—maintenance, not fireworks.

‘That’s not spiritual ambition,’ Joshua said. ‘That’s looking after the house. Houses need painting. Loves need speech. Ontologies need washing-up. You don’t rebuild the foundations every Tuesday. You also don’t lock the door and declare the building finished while the roof quietly leaks and the guests stand in a puddle being polite.’

Michael laughed, short and grateful. ‘So the bigger book is plumbing.’

‘Sacred plumbing,’ Joshua said. ‘Amen to U-bends. Glory to the trap that stops the smell coming back up. Unromantic. Necessary. Most of holiness is unromantic if you watch it at work.’

They stopped at a bridge. Michael looked down at his own face broken up by ripples—someone recognisable, someone unfinished, someone who wanted more pages without wanting a throne.

‘I don’t need R. to want the bigger book,’ he said slowly. ‘I need her to trust I won’t paper over hers. And I need myself to trust that wanting more pages isn’t a betrayal of the last line.’

‘The last line was weather,’ Joshua said. ‘Weather continues. Funny old life doesn’t mean *stop*. It means *don’t get solemn enough to lie*.’

A cyclist rang a bell; they stepped aside; the world remained a shared corridor. Michael took out his phone, not the notebook—the notebook could wait without becoming a shrine to waiting.

He texted R.: *Still want the bigger book. Still not writing it on top of yours. Canal approved this message. Joshua is present as witness and coffee mule.*

Her reply took long enough to be real, which is how you know a person is living a life that is not your draft:

*Keep your canal. Keep your pen. Keep your hands off my seal. See you Thursday.*

He showed Joshua the screen. Joshua raised his coffee like a toast that had earned its heat.

‘That’s a publishing contract better than most,’ Joshua said. ‘Clear rights. Clear territories. No small print about annexation.’

They walked back along the towpath, two brothers in a field without outside, one of them still writing with his feet—step, step, the quiet rhythm of a man who would not stop and would not trample.

A duck that had won the argument with the man paddled past in triumph. Michael finished his coffee. The notebook stayed in his pocket, closed on purpose, hunger named, crusade refused.

The book, which had never been only paper, consented to another chapter without demanding a throne, a parade, or the right to edit anyone else’s ending.
