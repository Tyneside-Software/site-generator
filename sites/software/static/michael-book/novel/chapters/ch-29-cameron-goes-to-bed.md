# Chapter 29
## Cameron Goes To Bed

Friday night closed systems week with a living room, three mugs, and Cameron's spectacular refusal to be converted.

They had gathered at Michael's place—Joshua on the armchair that sagged in a theological way, Cameron on the sofa with his shoes neatly off, Michael pacing until Joshua pointed at a chair and said, 'Horizontal beam.'

The conversation tried to be light. It failed in the productive way.

Michael, chastened by yesterday's motte-and-bailey pin, still wanted one more pass at definitions. 'When I say both/and, I don't mean mush. When I say experience is primary, I don't mean brains are fake. When I say ^& is funny—'

'—you sometimes mean it is also load-bearing,' Cameron finished. 'I know. Definition fallacy is the trap. You redefine a word mid-argument until the critic is punching fog. I'm not punching fog tonight. I'm naming the move.'

Joshua sipped tea. 'He's right when you're slippery. You're right when he's reductive. The game is noticing which costume you're wearing.'

Cameron set his mug down with ceremonial finality. 'Here is my costume. Forty-two. Ordinary answers. Sleep. I will sandpaper your cathedrals when they start collecting tithes in the form of everyone else's awe. I will not follow you into every hatch. That is not betrayal. That is friendship with a circadian rhythm.'

Michael felt the week assemble behind him: late brains, marmalade, light's dual résumé, vacuum invoices, zero's hotel, imaginary bills, ant hills, whiteboards of maybes, the Sisyphus night, different speeds, plank in the eye, keys six and seven, the essay that overshot. All of it had been real. None of it required Cameron to stay up and clap.

'Thank you,' Michael said, and meant it without residue. 'For the sandpaper. For going to bed. For not letting me crown a keyboard. For holding forty-two like a seatbelt I can borrow when speed turns into fear.'

Cameron blinked, disarmed by sincerity. 'Well. Don't get soppy. I'll become unbearable.'

'Too late,' Joshua said fondly. 'You're already unbearable. That's the point.'

They laughed. The laugh was the true closing ceremony.

Cameron stood, stretched, checked the time—ten forty—and nodded as if the clock had signed a treaty. 'I'm going. If the universe ends overnight, text me in the morning. If it doesn't, also text me, but after nine.'

At the door he paused, hand on the frame. 'Michael. You're not wrong that things are stranger than the slogans. You're wrong when strangeness becomes a hierarchy with you near the top by pure noticing. Keep the noticing. Drop the ladder.'

'I'll try.'

'Trying is a speed. I'm still at forty-two. Goodnight.'

He left. His footsteps on the stair were ordinary and therefore perfect.

Joshua stayed long enough to wash his mug. 'You needed him this week as much as the physics.'

'More. Physics doesn't go to bed at you.'

'Amen.'

After Joshua left, Michael stood in the quiet flat and felt no urge to open a hatch. He washed the remaining mugs. He put Cameron's coaster back in the stack. Steam rose from the sink and vanished without a moral. He brushed his teeth. He set an alarm that was not a metaphysical claim. In the bathroom mirror he checked for planks, found only tired eyes, and accepted the diagnosis.

Tomorrow would bring whatever Act II still owed him—logic doors, masks, wolves, fonts waiting in the wings—but tomorrow was not allowed to steal tonight's small completion.

He thought of different speeds on the same road and smiled at the ceiling in the dark. Fast had its place. Forty-two had its place. Amen had its place. Bed had its place, and refused to be ranked beneath revelation.

Week two had done its job. Systems had been met without becoming tyrants. Cameron had been introduced properly—not as a villain of doubt, but as the patron saint of bedtimes and precise objections, the friend who refined by refusing, the sandpaper Michael had needed enough to thank out loud.

Somewhere a keyboard held six and seven like neighbours who shared a fence and no destiny.

Somewhere a brother said amen in his sleep, or near enough.

Somewhere Cameron reached a pillow at a human hour and completed, without drama, the most underrated spiritual practice available to mammals:

he went to bed.

Michael followed, slower, still himself, still noticing—

—and for once, willing to notice tomorrow.
