# Chapter 63
## God Has / Is / Maybe

“I need the wave uncollapsed,” Michael said.

They were back by the river, because water understands superpositions better than conference tables. Joshua skipped a stone; it hopped twice and failed, which is still a career. Rings spread, argued with each other, and settled into the larger argument of the current.

“Has. Is. Maybe,” Michael said. “People want one verb for God and a fistfight for the runners-up. *Has* a creation, *is* the field, *maybe* approaches in experience without a warrant. If I crown only *is*, I get accused of erasing personhood and prayer. If I crown only *has*, I smuggle an outside owner again. If I crown only *maybe*, I get a shrug that never risks love.”

Joshua dusted his hands. “So both-and with three hands.”

“Three hands,” Michael agreed. “The monogram didn’t force a single altar. The axes didn’t force a single direction. The fuck-life couplet didn’t force active or passive. This is the same muscle. Uncollapsed wave. You both did and didn’t, in different offices of language.”

A rower slid past, neat as a good distinction. Oars kissed water and left it for the next stroke. Nothing insisted the river choose a single story about what wetness meant.

Joshua: “Bookstore plain.”

Michael tried. “God has—if has-language helps you love and thank and not treat people like spare parts of a machine. God is—if is-language keeps you from inventing a landlord beyond being. God maybe—if maybe-language keeps you humble when experience goes quiet. The wave doesn’t have to collapse for you to make tea.”

“Phew,” Joshua said, and they both laughed, the laugh of men putting down a stone without pretending the hill is gone.

Michael sent Cameron the three verbs like a peace offering disguised as a report:

*Has / is / maybe. Uncollapsed. Three offices. No fistfight required.*

Cameron: *This is the most reasonable you’ve been while still being insufferable. I can live here.*

Michael: *High praise.*

Cameron: *It’s the only praise. Don’t spend it all at once.*

They walked under bridges where graffiti offered simpler theologies in spray that would outlast several governments and still lose to damp. Michael felt fond of simplicity without surrendering complexity—another uncollapsed pair.

“I used to think maybe was cowardice,” he said. “Now it feels like manners toward mystery. Not the maybe of never landing. The maybe of not kidnapping God into one grammar.”

“Land where you must,” Joshua said. “Ethics. Care. The no-sin refusal of exile. The dual altar honesty. Those can land. The metaphysical pronoun can stay a chord instead of a single note.”

Michael watched light on water—particle if you liked particle stories, wave if you liked wave stories, wet if you fell in. A swan ignored them with aristocratic skill. “I both did and didn’t prove it,” he said. “Frame-dependent. Novel as long recognition. Cameron’s court still open for business.”

“And your court?” Joshua asked.

“Open for living,” Michael said. “Which is a different docket.”

Evening at the desk, he wrote the three words without circling a winner. HAS. IS. MAYBE. Under them, not as a slogan for strangers but as a note to his own future panic: *uncollapsed on purpose.*

He thought of readers who would need one door more than another. He hoped the book could be a building with several doors, light on when open, no ticket for worthiness—like a certain hall with floor polish and broken crayon animals. Some would enter through thank-you. Some through the closed map. Some through the honest quiet when experience goes flat and false trumpets would be a kind of violence.

Joshua texted: *Tomorrow you wake the rest of us. Gently.*

Michael: *Gently. Also firmly. Both.*

Joshua: *Always both.*

Michael turned off the lamp. In the dark the three verbs glowed like afterimages—not competing neon, shared phosphorescence. He practised saying them without ranking, the way a musician hums a chord instead of three rival solos. Has—for the thank-you that needs an addressee. Is—for the closed map that will not reinstall a beyond. Maybe—for the nights when honesty refuses to fake a trumpet.

Cameron again, late: *Biscuits still on for the wake-up chapter?*

Michael: *Biscuits and an empty chair and no forced collapse.*

Cameron: *I can work with that.*

God has.

God is.

God maybe.

Phew.

The stone of forced choice stayed on the riverbed where Joshua’s skip had ended, and the river—having no outside to escape to—continued anyway, which was its own kind of amen: not a settled verb, a moving one, carrying light and rubbish and the occasional swan toward a sea that was still, somehow, only more of the same field wearing a saltier coat.
