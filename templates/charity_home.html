Why I’m doing this

I want to put a little prosperity back into the North East — somehow. I don’t want to give money once. I want it to do something useful: two hours of peace, and paid hours for a cleaner.

A cleaner, when things were falling apart

I’ve had rough times. When everything around me is falling apart, I sometimes struggle to prioritise the little things. At one of those points, not keeping the house clean started to cause real friction with relatives. So I hired a cleaner.

It’s one of the highest-impact supports I’ve ever had — not flashy, just practical dignity when I needed it.

After my children were born

When I think about when that support would have mattered most, I land on the weeks after each of my children arrived — Lewis, Katie, and Harriet. A whirlwind every time: everyone coming to see the baby, nobody leaving you alone, sleepless nights, no rest.

For just two hours, I want to give new parents some of that time back — to relax, and to be with their baby. That’s the welcome-home clean.

How it multiplies

Local cleaners get paid work. The evening software group gets a real thing to support. We start in Howden Ward, and we keep doing the work.

The longer personal ground

There is more under this than a single rough patch or one newborn week — childhood coping that stuck, a long self-engineered path I am only now fully seeing, and a choice to walk through the door with open eyes. That story lives on this page.

I’m choosing to walk through that door

I had a bit of a rough childhood with older children, and I learned coping techniques that stuck. Recently I had a moment where I realised I had tricked myself into getting where I am now — over a really long time — and I still have no idea how deep it all goes.

I only just figured it out by talking so much about why I am doing what I am doing. By working back through the probability tree, I’m almost working back through my life. It’s a journey where I am just following where it goes and seeing where it takes me.

I have been watching this video and it really resonated — what he said about training for life, not for a race. I’m choosing to walk through that door. Here goes.
