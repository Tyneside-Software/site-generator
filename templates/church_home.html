The domain is church.
The idea is a table.
More than one faith in the room.

Michael bought tyneside.church on a whim — no business plan, no building, no hymn sheet. What he actually wants is multi-faith discussion, for his own education. He has been talking with local church people, and with friends from other religions. That is the whole seed. Maybe it becomes a monthly meetup. Maybe it stays conversations.

Honest status

Informal talks only. No calendar. No venue booked. No mailing list. Not a registered church, charity, or place of worship. Nobody is being recruited. The next useful step is still just showing up to conversations — and, if people want it, trying one evening a month.

A reading — first page, last page, then jump

Michael wanted to stand up in church and read the bookends of the Bible in accessible English, then keep going — not Genesis-to-maps in a row, but the big ideas first, with the exciting bits mixed through, until every page is done.

That reading lives at tyneside.church/bible. The whole Catholic Douay-Rheims canon is on the site. Where we have house modern English, it is labelled; every other chapter is the Douay-Rheims (Challoner) until we catch up. Genesis 1 and Apocalypse 22 are the locked house wording. Audio on the bookends for now.

Why “church” if it is multi-faith?

Because that was the domain that was sitting there, and whims get written down in this family instead of forgotten. The name is not a claim that Tyneside is starting a denomination.

Education, not a pulpit

The point is to learn — how people actually believe, pray, argue, and live — from people who already do it. Church folk locally. Friends who practise other faiths. Listening first. Not a Tyneside sermon with visitors in the pews.

Already happening, quietly

This is not “we should talk to religious people someday”. The conversations have started. They are not a programme. This page exists so that if a monthly table makes sense, there is a door to point at — and if it doesn’t, the whim is still honest.

What that would look like (if it happens)

A small, regular gathering on Tyneside: church, mosque, gurdwara, temple, synagogue, humanist, “I’m not sure” — whoever will sit still and speak in good faith. No debate-club scoring. No converting the table. Tea, questions, going home knowing a little more than last month.

Place, time, and host rules are not set. No room is booked. WhatsApp if you would actually come.

What this is not

Not a church service

No Sunday offering, no membership roll, no claim to sacraments. If you need a church, go to one that already exists and has people in it.

Not a takeover of anyone’s tradition

Friends and local church people keep their own houses. Tyneside would only hold a table, if a table is wanted.

Not a book tour

This door is for sitting with living people, not for parking a thesis.

Where it sits

On the group sketchbook with the other whims that grew a domain. Charity is care in hours. Academy is exams. Church would be conversation — if conversation is what people show up for.
